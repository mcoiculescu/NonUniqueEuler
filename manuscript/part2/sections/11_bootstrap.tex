\section{Completion of the Bootstrap}
\label{sec:bootstrap}

We now pass from the seed to ring $1$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:relay-step}.
The time bound and the nonzero moment of ring $1$ will then give
Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction} by the compactness criterion of Part~I.
We use the setting and bootstrap assumptions of Section~\ref{sec:relay}.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:bootstrap}
Assume \textup{(N1)}--\textup{(N3)} and \textup{(P0)}--\textup{(P6)}. Then for
every $A_1>0$ and every $n\ge2$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)}]
\item $\Tend=T_1$; that is, the clock is defined up to level $1$. Moreover
  $\ksh_1\le\Kup s_1^5$;
\item the conclusions of Theorem~\ref{thm:active} hold for ring $1$ on
  $\Phase_1$, in ring-$1$ units and active time, with $G=G_1$,
  $\kappa=\ksh_1$, the constant $C_0$ and $\eta_b=\eta_{b,1}$; and
  $|c_1(T_1)|e^{\sigma_1\hat T_1}\ge\frac{15}{16}\delta_0$;
\item $D_2(T_1)\subset B_{a/8}$;
\item $T_1\le\Tstar$, with $\Tstar$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:time}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\emph{The seed.}
At $t=0$, $\tsh_n=0$, $G_n=0$, and $w_n(0)=\Phi^{\kseed}-\Phi$ in ring-$n$
units. By ($\kappa$6), $\kseed\le\kL$. Lemma~\ref{lem:seed} and (P4) give
\[
S_n(0)\subset\NNm_n,\qquad
\tfrac12\kseed\le\ksh_n\le2\kseed,\qquad
\|w_n(0)\|_{H^{M-1}}\le\Cseed\kseed\le C_0\ksh_n.
\]
By ($\kappa$1), $2\kseed<\delta_0$, so the clock is defined up to level $n$.
The switch time of the transient is positive for every $G$ since
$2^{3/2}C_0>1$, so $\rhohat_n(0)=C_0$. With $s\le M-1$ and $C_2\ge1$ the
active size at the initial time satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:boot-start}
\|w_n(0)\|_{H^s}\le C_0\ksh_n
<C_2\hat a_n(0)(1+\rhohat_n(0)).
\end{equation}
For $j<n$, the initial support $S_j(0)$ is contained in $\NNm_j$. Also
$D_{j+1}(0)\subset\overline B_{5r_{j+1}/4}\subset B_{ar_j/8}$ and the outer
rings lie outside $B_{6r_j}$. These separations define $h_j(0)$ and give
$\maj_j(0)=0$. The same holds for $h_n(0)$ and $\maj_n(0)$, with an empty
inner region. Thus the improved bounds hold at $0$.

\emph{Improvement through a fixed time.}
Fix $t\in[0,\Tend]$ with (BA)$(t)$. Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:relay-step}
first to the seeded interval. If $t>T_n$, take $k=n-1$ in the relay step.
After a completed interval ending at $T_{k+1}<t$, the improved bounds,
activation amplitudes and active estimates supplied by that interval are
the hypotheses for the next level $k$. This is the locomotive induction
shown in Figure~\ref{fig:relay-step}. If the current interval reaches
$t$, we stop there, including the hand-off when $t=\tsh_k$. Otherwise the
clock conditions have been verified
through $k$ and we proceed to $k-1$. There are at most $n$ levels, and if
we reach $T_1$ then $t\le\Tend=T_1$. This proves the improved bounds through
$t$, the amplitude bounds at the activations reached by $t$, and the active
estimates on the positive-length intervals reached by $t$.

\emph{Continuation in time.}
Let $t_*$ be the supremum of the times in $[0,\Tend]$ at which (BA) holds.
The seed gives (BA)$(0)$, and (BA) holds at every time below $t_*$ by its
interval definition. The flow is continuous on $[0,\Tend]\times\R^2$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:smooth}(2),(4). If a compact support lies in an open set at
one time, its image remains in that set for nearby times. When
$S_k(v)\subset N_k$, $w_k(v)$ is continuous in $H^s$ because
$\omega(v)\mathbf 1_{S_k(v)}=f^{(n)}_k\circ\flow_v^{-1}$
(Equation~\eqref{eq:omega-ge}) is smooth with support in a fixed compact
set on a bounded time interval. When $h_j$ is defined, it and its Duhamel
bound are continuous by Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}(1).

For an active ring, write
$R_k(v)=2C_2\hat a_k(v)(1+\rhohat_k(v))$ for the right side of (BA3).
It is continuous except at $v=\tsh_k+t_1(C_0,G_k)/A_k$, where the transient
jumps from a left limit of at most $2^{-3/2}$ to $1$, by the definition of
$t_1$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:active}. Thus $R_k$ is right-continuous, and at
every point with a left limit its value is at least that limit.

We first obtain (BA) at $t_*$. If $t_*>0$, a ring that is not debris at
$t_*$ was not debris earlier. Its support is in the closed annulus
$\Nflat_j$ at $t_*$ by continuity, so (BA1) holds. Every activation strictly
before $t_*$ has its lower amplitude bound by the relay step at earlier
times. Lemma~\ref{lem:geometry} then defines the fields through $t_*$ for
all rings quiet at that time. Continuity gives (BA2) there.

For a ring active at $t_*$ with $\tsh_k<t_*$, the left limit bound for
$R_k$ and continuity of $w_k$ give (BA3) at $t_*$. This proves (BA)$(t_*)$.

Suppose $t_*<\Tend$. The relay step gives the improved bounds at $t_*$,
including the bound at any activation there. Choose $\Delta>0$ with
$t_*+\Delta\le\Tend$ and no assigned activation or saturation time in
$(t_*,t_*+\Delta]$. The rings that are not debris on this interval were not
debris at $t_*$. Their supports are compact subsets of
$\NN_j\subset\operatorname{int}\Nflat_j$, so (BA1) persists for small $\Delta$.
All activations before $t_*+\Delta$ have their lower amplitude bounds,
including any at $t_*$. Lemma~\ref{lem:geometry} gives the fields for the
quiet rings through $t_*+\Delta$. Their Duhamel bounds are at most
$\Abar\varepsilon_j$ at $t_*$, so (BA2) persists by continuity. Any ring
active after $t_*$ in this interval is active at $t_*$ and has the strict
margin
\[
\|w_k(t_*)\|_{H^s}\le C_2\hat a_k(t_*)(1+\rhohat_k(t_*))<R_k(t_*).
\]
Continuity of $w_k$ and right-continuity of $R_k$ give (BA3) for small
$\Delta$. Since there are finitely many rings, one $\Delta>0$ works for all
of them, contradicting the definition of $t_*$. Thus $t_*=\Tend$ and the
improved bounds hold through $\Tend$.

\emph{The final ring and the elapsed time.}
If the clock stopped at $k_{\mathrm s}<n$, then
$\Tend=\tsh_{k_{\mathrm s}}$ would be reached under (BA). The hand-off in
Proposition~\ref{prop:relay-step} verifies the support and amplitude
conditions at that same time. This contradicts the failure at
$k_{\mathrm s}$ in the fixed clock. Hence $\Tend=T_1$.
The last relay step gives $\ksh_1\le\Kup s_1^5$, the active estimates
through $T_1$ with their saturation bound, and $D_2(T_1)\subset B_{a/8}$
since $r_1=1$.

For each $k<n$, the lower bound on $\ksh_k$ gives
\[
T_k-T_{k+1}
=\frac{\Tpin}{A_{k+1}}+\frac{\hat T_k}{A_k}
\le\frac{1}{A_k}\left(\Tpin+
\frac{\log_+(\delta_0/\Klo)+5\log(1/s_k)}{\sigma'}\right).
\]
The numerator grows at most linearly in $k$, while $A_k=A_1\mu^{k-1}$
grows geometrically. The seed contributes at most
$\log(2\delta_0/\kseed)/(A_n\sigma')$. Summing these bounds as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:time} gives $T_1\le\Tstar$ uniformly in $n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:reduction}]
Assume \textup{(N1)}--\textup{(N3)}. By Lemma~\ref{lem:nonempty}, we may choose
parameters satisfying (P0)--(P6). This fixes $\mu\in(1,\mu_{\max}]$ with
$\mu_{\max}\le\frac{17}{16}$ (by (P2)) and $\sbar\in(0,a/16]$ (by (P6) and
(B1)), and none of (P0)--(P6) involves $A_1$. Let $A_1>0$, let $f$ be the
function of Definition~\ref{def:datum} with these parameters, and let $\Tstar$ be the number of
Lemma~\ref{lem:time}. By Proposition~\ref{prop:integrability}, $f$ is radial
and nonnegative, $f\in (L^1\cap L^q)(\R^2)$ for every $q\in[1,\infty)$, and
$f\notin L^\infty(\R^2)$. The bracket in the definition of $\Tstar$ is a finite
sum of the term $1$ and of nonnegative terms, because $\Tz>0$, $\mu>1$,
$0<\sbar\le a/16<1$ and $\kseed<\delta_0$ by ($\kappa$1) at $\kappa=2\kseed$;
so $\Tstar\in[1/A_1,\infty)$, and in particular $\Tstar\in(0,\infty)$.

Fix $\chiout\in C_c^\infty((a/4,3))$ with $0\le\chiout\le1$ and $\chiout=1$ on
$[a/2,2]$, and put $\Psione(x)=\chiout(|x|)\bar x^3$; it does not depend on
$n$. The cutoff $\chiout$ is chosen so that, at the time $T_1$ below, it equals
$1$ on the support of ring $1$ and vanishes on the material inside ring $1$; so
the moment $\int\omega^{(n)}(T_1)\Psione$ sees only ring $1$.

For $n\ge2$ let $f^{(n)}$ and $\omega^{(n)}$ be as in Definition~\ref{def:approx},
with the seed size $\kseed$ of (P5), which lies in $(0,\kL]$ by ($\kappa$6) at
$\kappa=2\kseed$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:adm}, $(f^{(n)},\omega^{(n)})_{n\ge2}$ is a
$2$-admissible sequence for $f$ on $[0,\infty)$ (indexed from $n=2$), and
$\omega^{(n)}(t)$ is $3$-fold rotationally symmetric for every $n$ and $t$.
For $n\ge2$ put $T^{(n)}=T_1$, which is defined by
Proposition~\ref{prop:bootstrap}(1) and lies in $[0,\Tstar]$ by
Proposition~\ref{prop:bootstrap}(4). (SA) and (Det) hold by
Lemma~\ref{lem:nonempty}, and the clock is defined up to level $1$. By
Proposition~\ref{prop:bootstrap}(2), the conclusions of
Theorem~\ref{thm:active}(a)--(b) hold for ring $1$ on $\Phase_1$ with $G=G_1$,
$\kappa=\ksh_1$, the constant $C_0$ and $\eta_b=\eta_{b,1}=0$
(Definition~\ref{def:P6}). Ring $1$ has
$r_1=1$, so ring-$1$ units are the original lengths; at $t=T_1$ its active time
is $\hat T_1$, so $w(\hat T_1)=w_1(T_1)$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:active}. The
vorticity $\omega^{(n)}(T_1)$ is supported in $D_1(T_1)$, the disjoint union of
$S_1(T_1)$ and $D_2(T_1)$. By Proposition~\ref{prop:bootstrap}(3),
$D_2(T_1)\subset B_{a/8}$, so the part on $D_2(T_1)$ does not
contribute, as $\chiout$ vanishes on $[0,a/4]$. On $S_1(T_1)$,
$\omega^{(n)}(T_1)=A_1(\Phi+w_1(T_1))$ (Definition~\ref{def:regions}), which is
supported in $\NN\subset\{a/2\le|x|\le2\}$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:active}(a), where
$\chiout(|x|)=1$. As $\Phi$ is radial, $\pout_3[\Phi]=0$, with the
multipole moment $\pout_3$ of Definition~I.\ref{I-def:multipole}, so
$\int\omega^{(n)}(T_1)\Psione=A_1\int(\Phi+w_1(T_1))\,\bar y^3\dd y=2\pi A_1\,\pout_3[w_1(T_1)]$.

Write $c=c_1(T_1)$, $\lambda=\lambda_1$ and $\phist=\phist(G_1)$, the
unstable eigenfunction of Definition~I.\ref{I-def:eigendata}. We bound
$|\pout_3[w_1(T_1)]|$ from below by comparing $w_1(T_1)$ with
$2\Re(ce^{\lambda\hat T_1}\phist)$, as in Theorem~\ref{thm:active}\textup{(b)}. As
$\pout_3[\phist]=\pout_*(G_1)$ and $\pout_3[\overline{\phist}]=0$ (its integrand
carries the factor $e^{-6i\theta}$),
$\pout_3[2\Re(ce^{\lambda\hat T_1}\phist)]=ce^{\lambda\hat T_1}\pout_*(G_1)$. The
function $w_1(T_1)-2\Re(ce^{\lambda\hat T_1}\phist)$ is supported in $N$, because
$\Phi$, $\Phi+w_1(T_1)$ and $\phist\in X^0$ are. (P1) gives
$\Cp C_2\delta_0\le\cout/(16\pi)$, and ($\kappa$5) with $G=G_1\in[0,\Gone]$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:inner-circ} and (SA3)) and
$\kappa=\ksh_1$, which lies in $(0,\kseed]$ by
Proposition~\ref{prop:bootstrap}(1) and (B6) (it is positive since the clock is
defined up to level $1$, Definition~\ref{def:clock}), gives
$\Cp C_2(\rhohat_1(T_1)+\ksh_1{}^{1/2})\le\cout/(16\pi)$. As
$\hat a_1(T_1)=\delta_0$, Lemma~I.\ref{I-lem:uniform},
Theorem~\ref{thm:active}(b) with $\eta_b=0$ and these two inequalities give
\begin{align*}
\big|\pout_3[w_1(T_1)]-ce^{\lambda\hat T_1}\pout_*(G_1)\big|
&\le\Cp\big\|w_1(T_1)-2\Re(ce^{\lambda\hat T_1}\phist)\big\|_{H^s}\\
&\le\Cp C_2\delta_0\big[\rhohat_1(T_1)+\delta_0+\ksh_1{}^{1/2}\big]
\le\frac{\cout\delta_0}{8\pi}.
\end{align*}
As $G_1\in[0,\Gone]$, $|\pout_*(G_1)|\ge\cout/(2\pi)$ (Lemma~I.\ref{I-lem:uniform}), and
$|ce^{\lambda\hat T_1}|=|c_1(T_1)|e^{\sigma_1\hat T_1}\ge\frac{15}{16}\delta_0$
by Proposition~\ref{prop:bootstrap}(2). Hence
$|\pout_3[w_1(T_1)]|\ge\frac{\cout}{2\pi}\cdot\frac{15}{16}\delta_0-\frac{\cout\delta_0}{8\pi}
=\frac{11}{16}\cdot\frac{\cout\delta_0}{2\pi}\ge\frac{\cout\delta_0}{4\pi}$, and
multiplying by $2\pi A_1$ gives
$|\int\omega^{(n)}(T^{(n)})\Psione|\ge c_0:=\frac12A_1\cout\delta_0>0$. The number $c_0$
involves only $A_1$, $\cout$ and $\delta_0$, and $\Tstar$ only $A_1$, $\mu$,
$\sbar$, $\sigma'$, $\Tz$, $\delta_0$ and $\kseed$; none of them depends on $n$.
Moreover $\Psione$ has the form required in
Corollary~I.\ref{I-cor:defect}, because $\chiout\in C_c^\infty((a/4,3))$ is real.
Corollary~I.\ref{I-cor:defect} with $\bar T=\Tstar$ gives a renormalized weak
solution $\omega_\infty$ on $[0,\infty)$ with datum $f$, in the sense of
Definition~I.\ref{I-def:weak} with $q=2$, such that
$\omega_\infty\in C([0,\infty);L^p(\R^2))$ for every $p\in[1,\infty)$ and, for
every $t\ge0$, $\omega_\infty(t)$ is equimeasurable with $f$ and $3$-fold
rotationally symmetric, and a time $t_0\in[0,\Tstar)$ such that $\omega_\infty$ and $f$ are
distinct elements of $X_q(T')$ for every $T'>t_0$ and every $q\in(4/3,\infty)$.

Let $T>t_0$ and $q\in(4/3,\infty)$; then $T>0$. By
Lemma~I.\ref{I-lem:radial-stationary}, the stationary function $t\mapsto f$ is a
renormalized weak solution in $X_q(T)$ with datum $f$. Also $\omega_\infty\in
X_q(T)$, since, by equimeasurability, $\|\omega_\infty(t)\|_{L^r}=\|f\|_{L^r}$ for every
$r\in[1,\infty)$ and $\omega_\infty\in C([0,T];L^1\cap L^q)$. By
Definition~I.\ref{I-def:weak}, the restriction of $\omega_\infty$ to $[0,T)$
satisfies Equations~(I.\ref{I-eq:weak}) and (I.\ref{I-eq:renorm}) for every test function
$\varphi\in\DT$ and every renormalization $\Ren$; these identities involve only
$\omega_\infty$, its velocity $\KBS\ast\omega_\infty$ and the datum $f$, and not
the exponent of the space. So $\omega_\infty$ is a renormalized weak solution in
$X_q(T)$ with datum $f$, and, as $T>t_0$, the two solutions are distinct elements
of $X_q(T)$. This holds in particular for every $T>\Tstar$, since $t_0<\Tstar$.
So $f$, $\Tstar$, the stationary solution, $\omega_\infty$ and $t_0$ have every
property stated in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, for every $A_1>0$ with the same $\mu$
and $\sbar$, which were fixed before $A_1$.
\end{proof}
