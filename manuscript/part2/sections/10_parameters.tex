\section{Choice of Parameters}
\label{sec:param}

The relay requires a small seed, well-separated radii, and enough time for
the contribution of an active ring to determine the next hand-off.
These choices are compatible because they are made in a fixed order:
\[
\delta_0\quad\longrightarrow\quad\mu\quad\longrightarrow\quad\Tpin
\quad\longrightarrow\quad C_0\quad\longrightarrow\quad\kseed
\quad\longrightarrow\quad\sbar.
\]
Here $\delta_0$ is the amplitude at which a ring saturates, $\mu=A_{k+1}/A_k$
is the ratio of consecutive amplitudes, $\Tpin$ is the pinning duration in
kick time, and $C_0$ bounds the initial perturbation and inner field in
Theorem~\ref{thm:active}. The seed size is $\kseed$, and $\sbar$ fixes the radius
ratios $s_k=\sbar(2\mu)^{-k}$.

The exact requirements (P0)--(P6) used in the bootstrap are in
Appendix~\ref{sec:param-lists}.

First fix the ring, its spectral data and the regularity orders from
Sections~\ref{sec:single} and~\ref{sec:relay}. Choose $\delta_0>0$ small enough
that nonlinear errors are a small fraction of the outgoing moment. Then
choose $\mu>1$ close enough to $1$ that the kick dominates the subsequent
moment drift. With these fixed, choose $\Tpin$ large enough to make the
exponential tail of the kick small, and $C_0$ large enough for the seed and
field bounds in Theorem~\ref{thm:active}. The constants in those bounds are
already fixed when $C_0$ is chosen.

Next choose $\kseed$ small. This controls the errors accumulated before
activation and the transient part of the active evolution. The requirements
are imposed on every starting amplitude $0<\kappa\le2\kseed$, so the same
choice applies at every hand-off. Finally choose $\sbar$ small. This keeps
inner material confined, makes the spectral shifts $G_k$ small, and weakens
the feedback from the outer rings. Choosing it last also ensures that the
new amplitudes, of size $s_k^5$, fall within the seed range already fixed.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:nonempty}
Assume \textup{(N1)}--\textup{(N3)}. The choices (P0)--(P6) can be made in this
order, with all quantities finite and positive (except $\eta_{b,1}=\theta_1=0$),
and each quantity is determined by quantities fixed at earlier steps or earlier in the same step, as in
Table~\ref{tab:param}. None of the choices depends on $n$, $A_1$ or
$\omega^{(n)}$. Finally, if (P0)--(P6) hold, then for every integer $n\ge2$ and
every $A_1>0$ the standing assumptions (SA) hold with these parameters, and
(Det) holds.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first choices involve finitely many fixed constants. The interval for
$\sigma'$ is nonempty because $\sigb>211/2500>9/200$ and
$3\sigma/4\ge3\sigb/4>9/200$. We can therefore choose the regularity orders, then
$\delta_0$ small, $\mu-1$ small, $\Tpin$ large, and $C_0$ large in the stated
order. In particular $\mu$ is fixed before the seed is made small, so all
factors involving $(\mu-1)^{-1}$ are finite constants at the later stages.

The seed conditions either bound $\kappa$ by a fixed positive threshold or
bound expressions of the form
$C\kappa^\alpha\langle\log(\delta_0/\kappa)\rangle^N$ by fixed positive
constants, with $\alpha>0$ and $N\ge0$. The exponents are positive because
$\mu>1$ and $\sigma'<3\sigma/4$.
These expressions tend to zero as $\kappa\to0$. The active transient also
tends to zero at saturation, uniformly in $G\in[0,\Gone]$: its initial layer
has uniformly bounded length, while the saturation time grows like
$\log(1/\kappa)$ and its decay rate is at least $\sigb-\sigma'>0$.
Thus every condition holds throughout $(0,2\kseed]$ once $\kseed$ is small
enough.

After the seed is fixed, the radius conditions have the same form,
$C\sbar^\alpha\log^N(1/\sbar)$, except for spectral continuity and the
conditions imposed at all levels. Spectral continuity gives (Det) on the
shrinking interval $0\le G\le\mu\sbar^2$. For the conditions at all levels, the
confinement radius grows at most like $\log(1/s_k)$ and the outer feedback
has a factor $s_{k-1}^6$. Positive powers of $s_k$ dominate these logarithms,
uniformly for $s_k\le\sbar$. Hence one sufficiently small $\sbar$ works at
every level. The exact inequalities and these uniform estimates are checked
in Appendix~\ref{sec:param-verification}; they also give (SA) and (Det).
No choice uses $n$, $A_1$ or the solution $\omega^{(n)}$.
\end{proof}
