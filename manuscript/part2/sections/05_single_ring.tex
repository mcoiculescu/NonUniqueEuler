\section{Nonlinear Estimates for One Ring}
\label{sec:single}

In this section we study, in ring units, one ring of the approximate solutions: a
ring close to $\Phi$, whose deviation from $\Phi$ is driven by its own
nonlinearity and by the field $h$ of the vorticity inside and outside it, apart
from the point-vortex part of the inner vorticity, which enters the linearization
$\Lin(G)$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}). We prove three results. A ring that the
instability has not yet reached stays close to $\Phi$, and its deviation equals
the linear response to $h$ up to an error of second order
(Theorem~\ref{thm:quiet}). A ring whose component along
the unstable eigenfunction grows follows that eigenfunction until the envelope of
this component reaches a small size (Theorem~\ref{thm:active}). And the innermost ring of
the approximate datum can be given a small component along the unstable
eigenfunction by a rearrangement of $\Phi$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:seed}).
Sections~\ref{sec:relay} and~\ref{sec:bootstrap} apply Theorems~\ref{thm:quiet}
and~\ref{thm:active} to every ring, in its own units, and Section~\ref{sec:kick} also uses
Theorem~\ref{thm:quiet}. Lemma~\ref{lem:seed} gives the innermost ring of the
datum of Definition~\ref{def:approx}.

Throughout this section we assume \textup{(N1)}--\textup{(N3)}, we work in ring
units, and we use the objects of Definition~I.\ref{I-def:eigendata}: the unstable
eigenvalue $\lamst(G)$ of $\Lin(G)$ in the mode $3$ and its eigenfunction
$\phist(G)$, normalized in $L^2(\R^2)$; the functional $\ellst(G)$ that gives the
coefficient of a function along $\phist(G)$; the Riesz projection $\Riesz(G)$
onto the span of $\phist(G)$ and $\overline{\phist(G)}$; and the growth rates
$\sigma(G)=\Re\lamst(G)$ and $\sigb=\min_{G\in[0,\Gone]}\sigma(G)$. When $G$ is fixed
we write $\lamst=\lamst(G)$, $\phist=\phist(G)$, $\ellst=\ellst(G)$,
$\Riesz=\Riesz(G)$. The proofs of
Theorems~\ref{thm:quiet} and~\ref{thm:active} follow Grenier \cite{Grenier}: an
approximate solution of high order is built from the linear semigroup, and the order is so large that a crude energy estimate for the
remainder suffices.

We use the norms of $H^k$, $W^{1,\infty}$ and $C^k(N')$ fixed in
Section~I.\ref{I-sec:prelim}, and the embedding $\|f\|_{L^\infty}\le\|f\|_{H^2}$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:embed}\textup{(E2)}. A vector field $h$ on a neighborhood of $N'$
is \textit{$3$-fold rotationally symmetric} if $h(\Orot x)=\Orot h(x)$.

The equation for the deviation of a ring from $\Phi$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}\textup{(3)} below) assumes that the ring stays in
the annulus $N$, and the continuity arguments of this section keep it there by
bounding how far its material moves radially. We write $\omega_{\mathrm r}(0)$
for the initial vorticity of the selected ring, with $\mathrm r$ standing for
``ring''. Let $\flow$ be the flow
of a solution of Theorem~\ref{thm:smooth}, let $\omega_{\mathrm r}(0)$ have compact
support, let $\omega_{\mathrm r}(t)=\omega_{\mathrm r}(0)\circ\flow_t^{-1}$, and let
$\delta_{\mathrm r}(t)$ be the supremum of $\big||\flow_\tau(y)|-|y|\big|$ over
$\tau\in[0,t]$ and $y\in\supp\omega_{\mathrm r}(0)$ ($0$ if the support is empty);
it is a maximum if the support is nonempty, it is continuous and
nondecreasing, and $\delta_{\mathrm r}(0)=0$, because $(\tau,y)\mapsto\flow_\tau(y)$
is continuous. We say that \textit{every trajectory of $\omega_{\mathrm r}$ moves
radially by at most $\delta$ on $[0,t]$} if $\delta_{\mathrm r}(t)\le\delta$. Since
$\supp\omega_{\mathrm r}(\tau)=\flow_\tau(\supp\omega_{\mathrm r}(0))$, the support
of $\omega_{\mathrm r}(\tau)$, $\tau\le t$, then lies within radial distance $\delta$
of $\supp\omega_{\mathrm r}(0)$.

For $G\in[0,\Gone]$ and an integer $k\ge0$, $e^{t\Lin(G)}$ denotes the semigroup
of Lemma~I.\ref{I-lem:semigroup-construct} for the ring in ring units. It preserves
each mode, hence the subspace $X^k$ of the space $Y^k$ of that lemma
consisting of the functions whose mode components vanish outside $3\Z$. It maps
real-valued functions to real-valued functions, since it is the sum of a
Dyson-Phillips series built from the two operators of that lemma, which both do:
the transport group $e^{t\Lin^{\rm tr}}$, which rotates each circle $\{|y|=r\}$ by
the angle $\Omega(r;G)t$, and the coupling operator $\mathcal K$, here
$\mathcal Kw=-(\KBS\ast w)\cdot\nabla\Phi$. The approximate solutions of this
section are Duhamel integrals of this semigroup. The next lemma shows that they are
solutions of the inhomogeneous linear equation, differentiable in time as functions with
values in $X^{k-1}$ when the data lie in $X^k$.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:duhamel}
Let $G\in[0,\Gone]$, $T>0$, and let $k\ge1$ be an integer. Let $f_0\in X^k$ and
$g\in C([0,T];X^k)$, and put
$u(t)=e^{t\Lin(G)}f_0+\int_0^te^{(t-\tau)\Lin(G)}g(\tau)\dd\tau$. Then
$u\in C([0,T];X^k)\cap C^1([0,T];X^{k-1})$, and $\partial_tu=\Lin(G)u+g$ on
$[0,T]$. If $f_0$ and $g$ are real-valued, so is $u$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We first prove the continuity of $u$ in $X^k$, and then its differentiability in
$X^{k-1}$, where the generator of the semigroup acts on $X^k$ as $\Lin(G)$.
The semigroup $S(t)=e^{t\Lin(G)}$ is strongly continuous on $X^k$ and, by the
uniform boundedness principle, bounded on $[0,T]$. So $\tau\mapsto S(t-\tau)g(\tau)$
is continuous into $X^k$: the argument given below for the continuity of
$(s,\tau)\mapsto S(s-\tau)H(\tau)$ in $X^{k-1}$ uses only the strong continuity of
$S$ and a bound on $\|S(t)\|$ for $t$ in a compact interval, and both hold in
$X^k$. Hence
$\int_0^tS(t-\tau)g(\tau)\dd\tau=\int_0^tS(\tau)g(t-\tau)\dd\tau$
is continuous in $t$ by dominated convergence, and $u\in C([0,T];X^k)$. Moreover
$X^k\hookrightarrow X^{k-1}$, and $S$ is a $C_0$-semigroup on $X^{k-1}$, since it
is one on $Y^{k-1}$ and $X^{k-1}=Y^{k-1}_{3\Z}$ is a closed invariant subspace,
both by Lemma~I.\ref{I-lem:semigroup-construct}\textup{(2)}. By
Lemma~I.\ref{I-lem:semigroup-construct}\textup{(2)}, the generator $A$ of $S$ on
$X^{k-1}$ (the part in $X^{k-1}$ of the generator on $Y^{k-1}$ of that lemma)
satisfies $X^k\subset D(A)$, $Ay=\Lin(G)y$ and $\|Ay\|_{X^{k-1}}\le C\|y\|_{X^k}$ for
$y\in X^k$; here $\Lin(G)$ maps $X^k$ into $X^{k-1}$ because it preserves modes
(Lemma~I.\ref{I-lem:sector}\textup{(2)}). Below, $\|\cdot\|$ is the norm of $X^{k-1}$ and
the operator norm on $X^{k-1}$.

All integrals below are Riemann integrals of continuous functions with values
in a Banach space; Fubini's theorem holds for them, because it holds after
applying any continuous linear functional. We extend $g$ by $g(t)=g(T)$ for
$T<t\le2T$. Since each $t\mapsto S(t)x$, $x\in X^{k-1}$, is continuous, hence bounded on $[0,2T]$, the
uniform boundedness principle gives $M_T:=\sup_{0\le t\le2T}\|S(t)\|<\infty$.

Let $x\in D(A)$. For $t\ge0$,
$\epsilon^{-1}(S(\epsilon)-I)S(t)x=S(t)\epsilon^{-1}(S(\epsilon)x-x)\to S(t)Ax$, so $S(t)x\in D(A)$,
$AS(t)x=S(t)Ax$, and $S(t)Ax$ is the right derivative of $t\mapsto S(t)x$. For
$0<\epsilon\le t\le2T$,
$\epsilon^{-1}(S(t)x-S(t-\epsilon)x)-S(t)Ax=S(t-\epsilon)\big(\epsilon^{-1}(S(\epsilon)x-x)-Ax\big)+\big(S(t-\epsilon)-S(t)\big)Ax\to0$,
because $\|S(t-\epsilon)\|\le M_T$. Thus $t\mapsto S(t)x$ lies in $C^1([0,2T];X^{k-1})$ with
derivative $S(t)Ax$, and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:semigroup-FTC}
S(r)x-x=\int_0^rS(s)Ax\dd s\qquad(x\in D(A),\ 0\le r\le2T).
\end{equation}

Let $H\in C([0,2T];X^{k-1})$. The identity
$S(s'-\tau')H(\tau')-S(s-\tau)H(\tau)=S(s'-\tau')(H(\tau')-H(\tau))
+(S(s'-\tau')-S(s-\tau))H(\tau)$, whose first term has norm at most
$M_T\|H(\tau')-H(\tau)\|$ and whose second tends to $0$ by strong continuity,
shows that $(s,\tau)\mapsto S(s-\tau)H(\tau)$ is continuous on
$\{0\le\tau\le s\le2T\}$. Moreover
$s\mapsto\int_0^sS(s-\tau)H(\tau)\dd\tau$ is continuous on $[0,2T]$: for
$0\le s<s'\le2T$ the difference of its values is
$\int_s^{s'}S(s'-\tau)H(\tau)\dd\tau+\int_0^sS(s-\tau)(S(s'-s)-I)H(\tau)\dd\tau$,
of norm at most $M_T\sup\|H\|(s'-s)+2TM_T\sup_\tau\|(S(s'-s)-I)H(\tau)\|$; and the
operators $S(\epsilon)-I$, $0\le\epsilon\le2T$, are bounded by $M_T+1$ and tend strongly to
$0$ as $\epsilon\to0$, hence uniformly on the compact set $H([0,2T])$.

Now put $V(t)=\int_0^tS(t-\tau)g(\tau)\dd\tau$ and
$W(t)=\int_0^tS(t-\tau)Ag(\tau)\dd\tau$. Since
$\|A(g(\tau)-g(\tau'))\|\le C\|g(\tau)-g(\tau')\|_{X^k}$, $Ag$ is continuous
into $X^{k-1}$, so $W$ is continuous by the previous paragraph with $H=Ag$. By
Equation~\eqref{eq:semigroup-FTC} with $x=g(\tau)\in X^k\subset D(A)$ and Fubini's theorem,
\[
V(t)=\int_0^t\Big(g(\tau)+\int_\tau^tS(s-\tau)Ag(\tau)\dd s\Big)\dd\tau
=\int_0^t\big(g(s)+W(s)\big)\dd s ,
\]
so $V\in C^1([0,2T];X^{k-1})$ and $V'=g+W$. For $t\in[0,T]$ and $0<\epsilon\le T$ we have
$V(t+\epsilon)=S(\epsilon)V(t)+\int_t^{t+\epsilon}S(t+\epsilon-\tau)g(\tau)\dd\tau$, and $\epsilon^{-1}$ times the
last integral tends to $g(t)$ as $\epsilon\to0$, by the continuity of
$(s,\tau)\mapsto S(s-\tau)g(\tau)$ at $(t,t)$. Hence
$\epsilon^{-1}(S(\epsilon)V(t)-V(t))\to V'(t)-g(t)=W(t)$ in $X^{k-1}$.
By definition of the generator $A$, existence of this right difference-quotient
limit means that $V(t)\in D(A)$, and the limit is $AV(t)$; hence $AV(t)=W(t)$.
Now apply the semigroup differentiation argument immediately preceding
Equation~\eqref{eq:semigroup-FTC} to $x=f_0$, which is admissible since
$f_0\in X^k\subset D(A)$. It gives $S(t)f_0\in D(A)$ and
$\frac{\dd}{\dd t}S(t)f_0=AS(t)f_0=S(t)Af_0$.
Combining these facts, $u=S(\cdot)f_0+V$ lies in
$C^1([0,T];X^{k-1})$, $u(t)\in D(A)$ with $Au(t)=S(t)Af_0+W(t)$, and
$\partial_tu=S(\cdot)Af_0+g+W=Au+g=\Lin(G)u+g$, since $u(t)\in X^k$. Finally, $S(t)$
preserves real-valued functions.
\end{proof}

\emph{Remark.} The function $u(t)$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:duhamel} is the
\emph{first Duhamel iterate}, combining the free evolution of $f_0$ with the
response to the prescribed driving term $g$.

\subsection{The Perturbation Equation}

We start with the global smooth solution $\omega$ given by
Theorem~\ref{thm:smooth} for a real-valued, $3$-fold rotationally symmetric
initial vorticity $\omega_0\in C_c^\infty(\R^2)$. In the applications,
$\omega$ is a rescaled, and possibly time-translated, version of the smooth
approximate solution $\omega^{(n)}$ of Definition~\ref{def:approx}.
We split its initial datum as
$\omega_0=\omega_{\mathrm r}(0)+\omega_{\mathrm{inn}}(0)+\omega_{\mathrm{ou}}(0)$,
corresponding to the selected ring, the vorticity inside it and the vorticity
outside it, and transport all three components by the full flow.

Our principal analysis concerns $\omega_{\mathrm r}$, through its deviation
$w=\omega_{\mathrm r}-\Phi$ from the reference ring. The inner vorticity's
circulation $2\pi G$ enters the linearization $\Lin(G)$ as a point vortex at
the origin. The rest of its velocity field, together with the velocity field
of $\omega_{\mathrm{ou}}$, forms the field $h$ in the perturbation equation.
The later estimates use the separation between the rings to control $h$ as
a small perturbation on the selected ring; the inner and outer vorticities
themselves need not be small, and $h$ retains its role in driving the
instability.

In the approximate data, the initial ring supports are widely separated.
The components $\omega_\bullet(t)$ below follow the material transported
from these initial supports. Since the full flow is a diffeomorphism at every
time (Theorem~\ref{thm:smooth}\textup{(4)}), their transported supports remain
pairwise disjoint for all time, and the decomposition remains well-defined.
The quantitative radial separation required below is an additional
hypothesis on $[0,T]$, as is the condition that the selected ring stays in
$N$. The later confinement and bootstrap estimates verify these conditions
on the time windows needed for the relay and the non-uniqueness argument.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:reduction}
Let $\omega$ be a solution given by Theorem~\ref{thm:smooth} whose datum
$\omega(0)$ is $3$-fold rotationally symmetric, and let $\flow_t$ be its flow. Let
$\omega(0)=\omega_{\mathrm r}(0)+\omega_{\mathrm{inn}}(0)+\omega_{\mathrm{ou}}(0)$ with
pairwise disjoint supports, $\omega_{\mathrm r}(0)$, $\omega_{\mathrm{inn}}(0)$,
$\omega_{\mathrm{ou}}(0)$ each $3$-fold rotationally symmetric, and put
$\omega_\bullet(t)=\omega_\bullet(0)\circ\flow_t^{-1}$ for
$\bullet\in\{\mathrm r,\mathrm{inn},\mathrm{ou}\}$. Put
$G=\frac1{2\pi}\int\omega_{\mathrm{inn}}(0)$. Let $T>0$ and assume that for every
$t\in[0,T]$, $\supp\omega_{\mathrm{inn}}(t)\subset\overline B_{a/8}$ and
$\supp\omega_{\mathrm{ou}}(t)\cap B_6=\emptyset$. Put
\[
h(t)=\KBS\ast\big(\omega_{\mathrm{inn}}(t)+\omega_{\mathrm{ou}}(t)\big)-G\,\frac{x^\perp}{|x|^2}
\qquad\text{on a neighborhood of }N'.
\]
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)}]
\item for every $t\in[0,T]$, $h(t)$ is smooth, divergence-free and
  $3$-fold rotationally symmetric on a neighborhood of $N'$; and $t\mapsto h(t)\in C^k(N')$ is
  continuous on $[0,T]$ for every $k$;
\item $\omega_{\mathrm r}\in C^\infty(\R\times\R^2)$, and on $[0,T]\times N'$ the
  velocity $v=\KBS\ast\omega$ is
  \[
  v=\Omega(|x|;G)\,x^\perp+h+\KBS\ast(\omega_{\mathrm r}-\Phi),
  \]
  with $\Omega$ of Definition~I.\ref{I-def:sector-setting} for $(\Phi,G)$; consequently,
  if $t\in[0,T]$ and $\flow_\tau(y)\in N'$ for every $\tau\in[0,t]$, then
  $|\flow_t(y)|-|y|=\int_0^t\big((h+\KBS\ast(\omega_{\mathrm r}-\Phi))\cdot\er\big)(\tau,\flow_\tau(y))\dd\tau$;
\item if $\supp\omega_{\mathrm r}(t)\subset N$ for $t\in[0,T]$, then the function
  $w=\omega_{\mathrm r}-\Phi$ is real-valued, it lies in $C^1([0,T];X^k)$ for
  every $k$, and
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:ring}
  \partial_tw=\Lin(G)w+B(w,w)-h\cdot\nabla w-h\cdot\nabla\Phi,\qquad B(v,w)=-(\KBS\ast v)\cdot\nabla w,
  \end{equation}
  on $[0,T]\times\R^2$, where $h\cdot\nabla w$ and $h\cdot\nabla\Phi$ denote the
  functions equal to these products on $N'$ and to $0$ elsewhere.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $K_\bullet=\supp\omega_\bullet(0)$. These compact sets are pairwise
disjoint, so each $\omega_\bullet(0)$ agrees with $\omega(0)$ near $K_\bullet$ and
vanishes near the other two; hence $\omega_\bullet(0)\in C_c^\infty(\R^2)$. The
map $(t,x)\mapsto\flow_t^{-1}(x)$ is the value at time $0$ of the solution of
$\dot z=v(\tau,z)$ with $z(t)=x$. Since $v$ is smooth and bounded on
$[-T',T']\times\R^2$ for every $T'>0$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:smooth}\textup{(2)}),
Lemma~\ref{lem:flow}\textup{(a)}, \textup{(b)} (Appendix~\ref{app:smooth}) shows that this map
is defined and
$C^\infty$ on $\R\times\R^2$. Hence $\omega_\bullet\in C^\infty(\R\times\R^2)$,
$\supp\omega_\bullet(t)=\flow_t(K_\bullet)$, and $\int\omega_{\mathrm{inn}}(t)=2\pi G$
because $\flow_t$ preserves area. By Theorem~\ref{thm:smooth}\textup{(6)},
$\omega(t)$ is $3$-fold rotationally symmetric. Since $\KBS(\Orot x)=\Orot\KBS(x)$, we get
$(\KBS\ast g)(\Orot x)=\Orot(\KBS\ast g)(x)$ for every $3$-fold rotationally symmetric
$g\in (L^1\cap L^\infty)(\R^2)$; so $v(t,\Orot x)=\Orot v(t,x)$, uniqueness for the flow
gives $\flow_t(\Orot y)=\Orot\flow_t(y)$, and each $\omega_\bullet(t)$ is $3$-fold
rotationally symmetric.

\emph{Proof of (1).} By continuity of the flow on compact sets, and since
$\flow_t(K_{\mathrm{inn}})=\supp\omega_{\mathrm{inn}}(t)\subset\overline B_{a/8}$,
$\rho_{\mathrm{inn}}:=\max\{|\flow_t(y)|:t\in[0,T],\,y\in K_{\mathrm{inn}}\}\le a/8<a/4$
($\rho_{\mathrm{inn}}:=0$ if $K_{\mathrm{inn}}=\emptyset$), and $|\flow_t(y)|\ge6$ for
$t\in[0,T]$ and $y\in K_{\mathrm{ou}}$. So the open annulus
$U=\{\frac12(\rho_{\mathrm{inn}}+\frac a4)<|x|<4\}\supset N'$ has distance at least
$\min(\frac12(\frac a4-\rho_{\mathrm{inn}}),2)>0$ from
$\supp(\omega_{\mathrm{inn}}(t)+\omega_{\mathrm{ou}}(t))$ for $t\in[0,T]$. Since
$|\partial^\beta\KBS(z)|\le C_\beta|z|^{-1-|\beta|}$, and $\omega_{\mathrm{inn}}$,
$\omega_{\mathrm{ou}}$ are continuous with supports in a fixed compact set for
$t\in[0,T]$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:smooth}\textup{(1)}), we may differentiate under the
integral sign: $h(t)\in C^\infty(U)$, every $\partial^\beta_xh$ is continuous on
$[0,T]\times U$, and in particular $t\mapsto h(t)\in C^k(N')$ is continuous. The
fields $x\mapsto\KBS(x-y)=\nabla^\perp_x\big(\frac1{2\pi}\log|x-y|\big)$ and
$x^\perp/|x|^2$ are divergence-free away from $y$ and from $0$; hence
$h(t)$ is divergence-free on $U$, and it is $3$-fold rotationally symmetric by the rotation identities above and
$(\Orot x)^\perp=\Orot x^\perp$.

\emph{Proof of (2).} The radial Biot-Savart formula of
Lemma~I.\ref{I-lem:radial-stationary} applies to $\Phi$ and holds pointwise
away from $0$ by smoothness. Thus, by the definition of $h$, on $[0,T]\times U$,
\[
v=\KBS\ast\Phi+\KBS\ast(\omega_{\mathrm r}-\Phi)+\KBS\ast(\omega_{\mathrm{inn}}+\omega_{\mathrm{ou}})
=\Big(\frac{\Circ_\Phi(|x|)}{2\pi|x|^2}+\frac G{|x|^2}\Big)x^\perp+\KBS\ast(\omega_{\mathrm r}-\Phi)+h,
\]
and the bracket is $\Omega(|x|;G)$. Since $\partial_\tau\flow_\tau(y)=v(\tau,\flow_\tau(y))$
and $x^\perp\cdot\er=0$, we get $\frac{\dd}{\dd\tau}|\flow_\tau(y)|=
\big((h+\KBS\ast(\omega_{\mathrm r}-\Phi))\cdot\er\big)(\tau,\flow_\tau(y))$ while
$\flow_\tau(y)\in N'$, which gives the integral formula.

\emph{Proof of (3).} Since $\supp\Phi\subset\{a\le|y|\le1\}\subset N$
(Lemma~I.\ref{I-lem:ring-facts}), $w(t)$ is a smooth, real, $3$-fold rotationally symmetric function
supported in $N$. The derivatives $\partial^\alpha_xw$ and
$\partial_t\partial^\alpha_xw$ are uniformly continuous on the compact set
$[0,T]\times N$ and, for each $t\in[0,T]$, vanish for $x\notin N$.
Thus the difference quotients of
$t\mapsto w(t)$ converge in every $H^k$, and $w\in C^1([0,T];X^k)$.

The ring component is transported by the full velocity $v$. Differentiating
its defining identity along a trajectory gives
\[
0=\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\omega_{\mathrm r}(t,\flow_t(y))
=\big(\partial_t\omega_{\mathrm r}+v\cdot\nabla\omega_{\mathrm r}\big)(t,\flow_t(y)).
\]
Since $\flow_t$ is onto, $\partial_t\omega_{\mathrm r}
+v\cdot\nabla\omega_{\mathrm r}=0$ at every spatial point.
Now $\omega_{\mathrm r}=\Phi+w$ and $\Phi$ is independent of time, so
$\partial_t\omega_{\mathrm r}=\partial_tw$ and
$\nabla\omega_{\mathrm r}=\nabla\Phi+\nabla w$.
Substituting the velocity decomposition from (2), on $[0,T]\times N'$ we obtain
\[
\partial_tw=-\big(\Omega(|x|;G)x^\perp+\KBS\ast w+h\big)
\cdot(\nabla\Phi+\nabla w).
\]
Because $\Phi$ is radial,
$x^\perp\cdot\nabla\Phi=\partial_\theta\Phi=0$, whereas
$x^\perp\cdot\nabla w=\partial_\theta w$. Expanding the product therefore gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\partial_tw
&=-\Omega(\cdot;G)\,\partial_\theta w-(\KBS\ast w)\cdot\nabla\Phi\\
&\quad-(\KBS\ast w)\cdot\nabla w-h\cdot\nabla w-h\cdot\nabla\Phi.
\end{aligned}
\]
The first line on the right is $\Lin(G)w$ by
Definition~I.\ref{I-def:sector-setting}, with background $\Phi$.
The self-interaction term $-(\KBS\ast w)\cdot\nabla w$ is $B(w,w)$, and
the remaining two terms describe advection of $w$ and $\Phi$ by $h$.
This is Equation~\eqref{eq:ring} on $N'$. The spatial support properties
above extend it to $\R^2$, with the products involving $h$ extended by zero
as in the statement.
\end{proof}

In the setting of Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}, put $w=\omega_{\mathrm r}-\Phi$. Then
$t\mapsto\|w(t)\|_{H^k}$ is continuous
on $[0,T]$ for every $k$, since $\omega_{\mathrm r}$ is smooth and its support stays
in a fixed compact set (Theorem~\ref{thm:smooth}\textup{(1)}). We fix once and for
all the auxiliary objects of the estimates below. The functions we estimate are
supported in $N$, and the velocities that act on them are needed only on a
neighborhood of $N$. We therefore multiply the Biot-Savart velocity by a compactly
supported cutoff $\chi_B$ that does not change it on $N'$, so that
Lemma~\ref{lem:Hk-calculus}\textup{(3)} applies, and we use a cutoff $\chi_N$ around $N$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:Hk-calculus}\textup{(1)}
and \textup{(2)}. Let $N_1=\{\frac a4<|y|<3\}$ be
the interior of $N'$. Fix $\chi_B\in C_c^\infty(B_4)$ with $\chi_B=1$ on $B_{7/2}$,
and $\chi_N\in C_c^\infty(N_1)$ with $\chi_N=1$ on a neighborhood of $N$.
The constants in the applications of Lemma~\ref{lem:Hk-calculus} below
depend only on the derivative order and $a$.

\newpage
In the analytic estimates that follow, $A\lesssim B$ means $A\le CB$ for
an auxiliary constant whose dependencies are specified in the argument.
Constants are uniform in $G\in[0,\Gone]$, the number of rings, their indices
and $A_1$, unless stated otherwise. Named constants are fixed before the
parameters whose choice depends on them.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:bilinear}
Let $k\ge2$ be an integer. There exist a constant $C_{\mathrm{bil}}(k)$,
depending only on $k$ and $a$, and an absolute constant
$C_{\mathrm{BS}}$, such that for $v,w\in X^{k+1}$ and every vector field
$h\in C^k(N';\R^2)$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)}]
\item $\|B(v,w)\|_{H^k}\le C_{\mathrm{bil}}(k)\|v\|_{H^k}\|w\|_{H^{k+1}}$;
\item $\|h\cdot\nabla w\|_{H^k}\le C_{\mathrm{bil}}(k)\|h\|_{C^k(N')}\|w\|_{H^{k+1}}$;
\item $\|h\cdot\nabla\Phi\|_{H^k}\le\Cnab\|h\|_{C^k(N')}$, with $\Cnab=\Cnab(k)$ of
  Lemma~I.\ref{I-lem:uniform};
\item $\sup_{|x|\le3}|(\KBS\ast v)(x)|\le C_{\mathrm{BS}}\|v\|_{H^1}$.
\end{enumerate}
The functions in \textup{(1)}--\textup{(3)} are supported in $N$, and they lie in
$X^k$ if $h$ is $3$-fold rotationally symmetric.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since $\nabla w$ is supported in $N$,
$B(v,w)=-\sum_{i=1,2}(\chi_B\KBS\ast v)_i\,\partial_iw$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:Hk-calculus}\textup{(2)} (with $\max(k,3)$ in place of its $k$, and
$j=k$) and \textup{(3)},
\[
\|B(v,w)\|_{H^k}\lesssim
\|\chi_B\KBS\ast v\|_{H^k}\|w\|_{H^{k+1}}
\lesssim\|v\|_{H^{k-1}}\|w\|_{H^{k+1}},
\]
with constants depending only on $k$ and $a$, which gives (1).
For (2), write $h\cdot\nabla w=(\chi_Nh)\cdot\nabla w$ and apply the
Leibniz rule. Each term is bounded by a constant depending only on $k$ and
$\chi_N$ times $\|h\|_{C^k(N')}\|w\|_{H^{k+1}}$.
Item (3) is the
last inequality of Lemma~I.\ref{I-lem:uniform}. For (4), $\KBS\ast v=\chi_B\KBS\ast v$ on
$B_{7/2}$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:embed}\textup{(E2)}, applied to each component, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:Hk-calculus}\textup{(3)} give
$\|\chi_B\KBS\ast v\|_{L^\infty}\lesssim\|v\|_{H^1}$ with an absolute constant,
which gives (4), since $\{|x|\le3\}\subset B_{7/2}$. The functions in
(1)--(3) vanish outside $\supp\nabla w\cup\supp\nabla\Phi\subset N$. If $v$, $w$ and
$h$ are $3$-fold rotationally symmetric, the vector fields $\KBS\ast v$,
$\nabla w$ and $\nabla\Phi$ satisfy $V(\Orot x)=\Orot V(x)$; for
$\KBS\ast v$ this was proved in Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}.
Their scalar products are therefore $3$-fold rotationally symmetric.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:energy} compares the deviation $w$ of a ring with an
approximate solution $w_{\mathrm{app}}$ by an energy estimate in $H^s$.
In the proofs of Theorem~\ref{thm:quiet} and Lemma~\ref{lem:window},
$w_{\mathrm{app}}$ will be a finite sum of iterated Duhamel terms, starting
with the linear response and adding nonlinear corrections as in
Equation~\eqref{eq:hier} below. The estimate controls the error of this
approximation by its initial error and its residual.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:energy}
Let $s\ge3$ be an integer. Fix a sufficiently large rate $\Lam(s)>0$,
depending only on $\Phi$, $\Gone$ and $s$, and assume in addition that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Lambda}
\Lam(s)\ge2\max_{G\in[0,\Gone]}\sigma(G).
\end{equation}
Let $G\in[0,\Gone]$
and $t>0$, and let the assumptions of Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}\textup{(3)} hold on
$[0,t]$, with $w=\omega_{\mathrm r}-\Phi$. Let
$w_{\mathrm{app}}\in C([0,t];X^{s+1})\cap C^1([0,t];X^s)$ be real-valued, and put
$v=w-w_{\mathrm{app}}$ and
\[
R_{\mathrm{app}}=\partial_tw_{\mathrm{app}}-\big(\Lin(G)w_{\mathrm{app}}+B(w_{\mathrm{app}},w_{\mathrm{app}})-h\cdot\nabla w_{\mathrm{app}}-h\cdot\nabla\Phi\big).
\]
If throughout $[0,t]$,
\[
\|w\|_{H^s}\le1,\qquad
\|w_{\mathrm{app}}\|_{H^{s+1}}\le1,\qquad
\|h\|_{C^{s+1}(N')}\le1,
\]
then for
$\tau\in[0,t]$,
\[
\|v(\tau)\|_{H^s}\le e^{\Lam(s)\tau}\|v(0)\|_{H^s}+\int_0^\tau e^{\Lam(s)(\tau-\tau')}\|R_{\mathrm{app}}(\tau')\|_{H^s}\dd\tau' .
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On $N_1$ put $U=\Omega(|x|;G)x^\perp+h+\KBS\ast w$, and for $f$ supported in $N$ let
$U\cdot\nabla f$ mean $(\chi_NU)\cdot\nabla f$.
Recall that $\Lin(G)w=-\Omega(|x|;G)\partial_\theta w-(\KBS\ast w)\cdot\nabla\Phi$
and $\partial_\theta w=x^\perp\cdot\nabla w$. Substituting this and
$B(w,w)=-(\KBS\ast w)\cdot\nabla w$ into the equation of
Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}\textup{(3)} gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\partial_tw
&=-\Omega(|x|;G)x^\perp\cdot\nabla w-(\KBS\ast w)\cdot\nabla\Phi
  -(\KBS\ast w)\cdot\nabla w-h\cdot\nabla w-h\cdot\nabla\Phi\\
&=-U\cdot\nabla w-(\KBS\ast w+h)\cdot\nabla\Phi.
\end{aligned}
\]
Moving the transport term to the left yields
$\partial_tw+U\cdot\nabla w=-(\KBS\ast w)\cdot\nabla\Phi-h\cdot\nabla\Phi$.
For the approximate solution, put
$U_{\mathrm{app}}=\Omega(|x|;G)x^\perp+h+\KBS\ast w_{\mathrm{app}}$.
The definition of $R_{\mathrm{app}}$ gives
\[
\partial_tw_{\mathrm{app}}+U_{\mathrm{app}}\cdot\nabla w_{\mathrm{app}}
=-(\KBS\ast w_{\mathrm{app}})\cdot\nabla\Phi-h\cdot\nabla\Phi+R_{\mathrm{app}}.
\]
Since $U-U_{\mathrm{app}}=\KBS\ast v$ and
\[
U\cdot\nabla w-U_{\mathrm{app}}\cdot\nabla w_{\mathrm{app}}
=U\cdot\nabla v+(\KBS\ast v)\cdot\nabla w_{\mathrm{app}},
\]
subtracting the equation for $w_{\mathrm{app}}$ from that for $w$ gives
\[
\partial_tv+U\cdot\nabla v=-(\KBS\ast v)\cdot\nabla(\Phi+w_{\mathrm{app}})-R_{\mathrm{app}},
\]
where every term lies in $C([0,t];H^s)$, and
$v\in C^1([0,t];H^s)\cap C([0,t];H^{s+1})$ is real and supported in $N$.

The field $U$ is divergence-free on $N_1$: $\divop h=0$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}\textup{(1)}, $\divop(\KBS\ast w)=0$, and
$\divop(f(|x|)x^\perp)=f'(|x|)\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot x^\perp=0$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:Hk-calculus}\textup{(3)}, Sobolev embedding and the hypotheses give
\[
\|U\|_{W^{1,\infty}(N_1)}+\|U\|_{H^s(N_1)}\lesssim1.
\]
Here the implicit constant depends only on $\Phi$, $\Gone$ and $s$:
$\Omega(r;G)=\Omega(r;0)+Gr^{-2}$ is uniformly smooth on
$[a/4,3]\times[0,\Gone]$. Since $U$ and $v$ are real and $\divop U=0$ on
$N_1$, Lemma~\ref{lem:Hk-calculus}\textup{(1)} bounds the transport contribution
by $C\|v\|_{H^s}^2$. Parts \textup{(2)} and \textup{(3)} of that lemma also give
\[
\|(\KBS\ast v)\cdot\nabla(\Phi+w_{\mathrm{app}})\|_{H^s}
\lesssim\|v\|_{H^s}
\big(\|\Phi\|_{H^{s+1}}+\|w_{\mathrm{app}}\|_{H^{s+1}}\big)
\lesssim\|v\|_{H^s}.
\]
Apply $\partial^\alpha$ to the equation of $v$, take the $L^2$ inner product
with $\partial^\alpha v$, and sum over $|\alpha|\le s$. Choosing $\Lam(s)$
large enough to dominate these bounds gives
$\frac12\frac{\dd}{\dd\tau}\|v\|_{H^s}^2\le\Lam(s)\|v\|_{H^s}^2+\|R_{\mathrm{app}}\|_{H^s}\|v\|_{H^s}$.
To divide by $\|v\|_{H^s}$, which may vanish, we regularize it. For $\epsilon>0$
the function $y_\epsilon=(\|v\|_{H^s}^2+\epsilon^2)^{1/2}$ is $C^1$, as
$v\in C^1([0,t];H^s)$ and $y_\epsilon\ge\epsilon$. Since
$y_\epsilon y_\epsilon'=\frac12\frac{\dd}{\dd\tau}\|v\|_{H^s}^2$ and
$\|v\|_{H^s}\le y_\epsilon$, it satisfies
$y_\epsilon'\le\Lam(s)y_\epsilon+\|R_{\mathrm{app}}\|_{H^s}$, so
$(e^{-\Lam(s)\tau}y_\epsilon)'\le e^{-\Lam(s)\tau}\|R_{\mathrm{app}}\|_{H^s}$.
Integrating from $0$ to $\tau$, and letting $\epsilon\to0$ with
$\|v\|_{H^s}\le y_\epsilon$ and $y_\epsilon(0)\to\|v(0)\|_{H^s}$, proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

\emph{Approximate solutions.} Following Grenier, we approximate the deviation $w$
by the first $J$ terms $w^{[1]},\dots,w^{[J]}$ of its formal expansion in powers of
the size of the data, in which $h$ and the initial value count as terms of first
order: $w^{[1]}$ solves the linear equation driven by $-h\cdot\nabla\Phi$, with an
initial value $w_0$ that is $0$ or $w(0)$ in our applications, and $w^{[j]}$,
$j\ge2$, solves the linear equation with initial value $0$, driven by the terms of
order $j$ of $B(w,w)-h\cdot\nabla w$. For example, the order-two term of this
nonlinear expression is $B(w^{[1]},w^{[1]})-h\cdot\nabla w^{[1]}$.
Spatial differentiation preserves the formal order: both $w^{[j]}$ and
$\nabla w^{[j]}$ have order $j$. Multiplying by the first-order field $h$
therefore makes $h\cdot\nabla w^{[j]}$ of order $j+1$. The background $\Phi$
is fixed and has order zero, so $h\cdot\nabla\Phi$ has order one and enters
the equation for $w^{[1]}$. The residual of the sum consists of terms of
order larger than $J$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:hier-res} below), and
Lemma~\ref{lem:energy} controls the difference
between $w$ and the sum. Let the assumptions of Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction} hold
on $[0,T]$, assume $G\in[0,\Gone]$, and put $\Lin=\Lin(G)$ and $F=-h\cdot\nabla\Phi$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}\textup{(1)} and Lemma~\ref{lem:bilinear}, $F$ is real
and lies in $C([0,T];X^k)$ for every $k$; so, by Lemma~\ref{lem:duhamel}, for every
real $w_0\in\bigcap_kX^k$ the function
$e^{t\Lin}w_0+\int_0^te^{(t-\tau)\Lin}F(\tau)\dd\tau$ is real, lies in
$C([0,T];X^k)\cap C^1([0,T];X^{k-1})$ for every $k\ge1$, and solves
$\partial_tu=\Lin u+F$. If $\supp\omega_{\mathrm r}(0)\subset N$, this applies to
$w_0=w(0)=\omega_{\mathrm r}(0)-\Phi$, which is then smooth
(Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}\textup{(2)}), real, $3$-fold rotationally symmetric and supported in $N$,
hence lies in every $X^k$. Let $w^{[1]}$ be such a function, let $J\ge1$ be an integer,
and for $2\le j\le J$ define recursively
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:hier}
w^{[j]}(t)=\int_0^te^{(t-\tau)\Lin}g^{[j]}(\tau)\dd\tau,\qquad
g^{[j]}=\sum_{i+l=j}B(w^{[i]},w^{[l]})-h\cdot\nabla w^{[j-1]},
\end{equation}
where here and below $i,l\ge1$ in the sums. By induction on $j$,
Lemma~\ref{lem:bilinear} (whose bounds, with the continuity of $t\mapsto h(t)$ in
every $C^k(N')$, give continuity in time) and Lemma~\ref{lem:duhamel} show that
$g^{[j]}$ and $w^{[j]}$ are real, $g^{[j]}\in C([0,T];X^k)$ and
$w^{[j]}\in C([0,T];X^k)\cap C^1([0,T];X^{k-1})$ for every $k\ge1$, and
$\partial_tw^{[j]}=\Lin w^{[j]}+g^{[j]}$.

\begin{lemma}[Residual of the approximate solution]
\label{lem:hier-res}
Let $w^{[1]},\dots,w^{[J]}$ be constructed above, and put
$w_{\mathrm{app}}=\sum_{j=1}^Jw^{[j]}$. Then $w_{\mathrm{app}}(0)=w_0$, and its
residual, as defined in Lemma~\ref{lem:energy}, is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:hier-res}
R_{\mathrm{app}}=-\sum_{i,l\le J,\,i+l>J}B(w^{[i]},w^{[l]})+h\cdot\nabla w^{[J]} .
\end{equation}
Every term on the right has formal order larger than $J$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The first-order equation is
$\partial_tw^{[1]}-\Lin w^{[1]}=F=-h\cdot\nabla\Phi$.
Summing it with the equations for $2\le j\le J$ gives
\[
\partial_tw_{\mathrm{app}}-\Lin w_{\mathrm{app}}
=F+\sum_{\substack{1\le i,l\le J\\i+l\le J}}B(w^{[i]},w^{[l]})
  -h\cdot\nabla\sum_{j=1}^{J-1}w^{[j]}.
\]
By bilinearity,
$B(w_{\mathrm{app}},w_{\mathrm{app}})=\sum_{1\le i,l\le J}B(w^{[i]},w^{[l]})$,
and $\sum_{j=1}^{J-1}w^{[j]}=w_{\mathrm{app}}-w^{[J]}$. Substituting these
identities into the definition of the residual yields
\[
R_{\mathrm{app}}=
\underbrace{F+h\cdot\nabla\Phi}_{=\,0}
-\sum_{i,l\le J,\,i+l>J}B(w^{[i]},w^{[l]})+h\cdot\nabla w^{[J]},
\]
which proves Equation~\eqref{eq:hier-res}. Thus the background-driving term
cancels because it is already included in the first-order equation.
The bilinear terms have order $i+l>J$, and the last term has order $J+1$.
Finally, $w^{[1]}(0)=w_0$ and $w^{[j]}(0)=0$ for $j\ge2$ give the initial value.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A Quiet Ring}

A ring is quiet while the instability has not reached it: it starts exactly at
$\Phi$, so its deviation is created by the field $h$ alone, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:quiet} shows that it stays small. We bound a norm of the field by a
function $\eta$ of time, and its effect by the Duhamel bound of $\eta$, defined
next. Since the semigroup bound of Proposition~I.\ref{I-prop:semigroup} grows like
$e^{\sigma(G)t}$, this integral controls, up to a constant factor, the linear
response to a driving term of size $\eta$.

\begin{definition}
\label{def:majorant}
Let $G\in[0,\Gone]$, $T>0$, and let $\eta\colon[0,T]\to[0,\infty)$ be integrable.
The \textbf{Duhamel bound} of $\eta$ is
$\maj(t)=\int_0^te^{\sigma(G)(t-\tau)}\eta(\tau)\dd\tau$, $t\in[0,T]$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:majorant}
Let $G$, $T$, $\eta$ and $\maj$ be as in Definition~\ref{def:majorant}. Then for
$0\le\tau\le t\le T$:
\textup{(M1)} $e^{\sigma(G)(t-\tau)}\maj(\tau)\le\maj(t)$;
\textup{(M2)} $\int_\tau^t\eta\le\maj(t)$;
\textup{(M3)} $\int_0^t\maj\le\maj(t)/\sigma(G)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $\sigma=\sigma(G)$, which is positive by Proposition~I.\ref{I-prop:SU}. We have
$\maj(t)=e^{\sigma(t-\tau)}\maj(\tau)+\int_\tau^te^{\sigma(t-v)}\eta(v)\dd v$, and the
integral is nonnegative, which gives (M1), and at least $\int_\tau^t\eta$, which
gives (M2). By (M1), $\maj(\tau)\le e^{-\sigma(t-\tau)}\maj(t)$; integrating over
$\tau\in[0,t]$ gives (M3).
\end{proof}

Recall that $\sigb=\min_{G\in[0,\Gone]}\sigma(G)>0$ is the uniform lower
bound on the unstable growth rate. In the next theorem, $Q$ stands for
``quiet'': $N_Q$ is the order of the approximate solution and $C_Q$ is the
constant in the bounds for a quiet ring; $\majst$ is the smallness threshold for the
Duhamel bound. The subscript $h$ labels the regularity indices used in the
field estimates: the perturbation is controlled in $H^{s_h}$ and the field
$h$ is assumed bounded in $C^{M_h}$. Taking $M_h=s_h+N_Q+1$ supplies the derivatives needed
by the successive Duhamel terms and the energy estimate. Section~\ref{sec:relay}
uses these bounds to control the velocity fields induced by quiet rings,
and Section~\ref{sec:bootstrap} keeps those rings close to $\Phi$ until activation.

The condition $N_Q\sigb\ge\Lam(s_h)$ ensures that truncation at order $N_Q$
overcomes the growth allowed by the energy estimate. Indeed, the residual
contains a factor $\maj^{N_Q}$, and (M1) gives, for $0\le\tau\le t$,
\[
e^{\Lam(s_h)(t-\tau)}\maj(\tau)^{N_Q}
\le e^{(\Lam(s_h)-N_Q\sigma(G))(t-\tau)}\maj(t)^{N_Q}
\le\maj(t)^{N_Q}.
\]
Thus the remainder retains the higher-order smallness needed to compare
$w$ with its linear response.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:quiet}
Assume \textup{(N1)}--\textup{(N3)}. Let $s_h\ge3$ and $N_Q\ge2$ be integers with
$N_Q\sigb\ge\Lam(s_h)$, and put $M_h=s_h+N_Q+1$. There are $\majst\in(0,1]$ and
$C_Q<\infty$, depending only on $(\Phi,\Gone,s_h,N_Q)$, such that the following
holds. Let $\omega$,
$\omega_{\mathrm r},\omega_{\mathrm{inn}},\omega_{\mathrm{ou}}$, $G$ and $h$ be as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction} on $[0,T]$, with $\omega_{\mathrm r}(0)=\Phi$ and
$G\in[0,\Gone]$, and put $w=\omega_{\mathrm r}-\Phi$. Let $\eta$ be measurable with
$\|h(t)\|_{C^{M_h}(N')}\le\eta(t)\le1$ on $[0,T]$, let $\maj$ be its Duhamel
bound, and assume $\maj\le\majst$ on $[0,T]$. Then for every $t\in[0,T]$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item $\supp\omega_{\mathrm r}(t)\subset\NNm$;
\item $\|w(t)-\wlin(t)\|_{H^{s_h}}\le C_Q\maj(t)^2$, where
  $\wlin(t)=\int_0^te^{(t-\tau)\Lin(G)}\big(-h(\tau)\cdot\nabla\Phi\big)\dd\tau$
  is the \textbf{linear response};
\item $\|w(t)\|_{H^{s_h}}\le C_Q\maj(t)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Throughout, $\sigma=\sigma(G)\ge\sigb$ and $\Lin=\Lin(G)$. We compare $w$ with the
approximate solution of Equation~\eqref{eq:hier} with $J=N_Q$ and $w^{[1]}=\wlin$,
bound its terms by powers of $\maj$, and close with Lemma~\ref{lem:energy} in a
continuity argument on the radial displacement and on $\|w\|_{H^{s_h}}$.

\emph{Constants.} Put $\sigma'_0=\frac12(\frac9{200}+\sigb)\in(\frac9{200},\sigb)$.
The semigroup constants of Proposition~I.\ref{I-prop:semigroup} and the
constants of Lemma~\ref{lem:bilinear}, through order $M_h$, are finite and
uniform in $G$. All implicit constants in this proof depend only on
$(\Phi,\Gone,s_h,N_Q)$. Put $k_j=M_h-j$ for $1\le j\le N_Q$, so that
$k_{N_Q}=s_h+1$ and $k_j+1=k_{j-1}$. The finite induction below gives the
hierarchy, residual and energy bounds with these dependencies. Choose $C_Q$
large enough to dominate the coefficients in the resulting norm bounds,
and then choose $\majst>0$ sufficiently small that
\[
\majst\le1,\qquad C_Q\majst\le\tfrac12,\qquad
\big(1+C_{\mathrm{BS}}C_Q/\sigb\big)\majst\le a/16.
\]

\emph{Approximate solution.} Let $w^{[j]}$, $1\le j\le N_Q$, be the functions of
Equation~\eqref{eq:hier} with $J=N_Q$ and $w^{[1]}=\wlin$ (the case $w_0=0$), so that
they are regular by Lemmas~\ref{lem:duhamel} and \ref{lem:bilinear}. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:bilinear}\textup{(3)},
$\|F(\tau)\|_{H^{M_h}}\lesssim\eta(\tau)$. We claim that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Q-hier}
\|w^{[j]}(t)\|_{H^{k_j}}\lesssim\maj(t)^j\qquad(t\in[0,T],\ 1\le j\le N_Q).
\end{equation}
For $j=1$,
$\|\wlin(t)\|_{H^{k_1}}\lesssim\int_0^te^{\sigma(t-\tau)}\eta(\tau)\dd\tau\le\maj(t)$.
Let $j\ge2$ and assume Equation~\eqref{eq:Q-hier} for all indices less than $j$.
Since $k_j+1=k_{j-1}\le k_l$ for $l\le j-1$, Lemma~\ref{lem:bilinear} gives
$\|g^{[j]}(\tau)\|_{H^{k_j}}\lesssim\maj(\tau)^j+\eta(\tau)\maj(\tau)^{j-1}$.
By (M1), $e^{\sigma(t-\tau)}\maj(\tau)^j\le e^{-(j-1)\sigma(t-\tau)}\maj(t)^j$, whose
integral over $[0,t]$ is at most $\maj(t)^j/((j-1)\sigb)$; and
$e^{\sigma(t-\tau)}\eta(\tau)\maj(\tau)^{j-1}\le\eta(\tau)\maj(t)^{j-1}$, whose integral
is at most $\maj(t)^j$ by (M2). With $\|e^{(t-\tau)\Lin}\|\lesssim e^{\sigma(t-\tau)}$ this
gives Equation~\eqref{eq:Q-hier} for $j$. Since $k_j\ge s_h+1$ and $\maj\le1$,
Equations~\eqref{eq:hier-res} and \eqref{eq:Q-hier} and Lemma~\ref{lem:bilinear} give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Q-res}
\|R_{\mathrm{app}}(\tau)\|_{H^{s_h}}\lesssim\big(\maj(\tau)^{N_Q+1}+\eta(\tau)\maj(\tau)^{N_Q}\big),
\end{equation}
and $\|w_{\mathrm{app}}\|_{H^{s_h+1}}\lesssim\maj$ on $[0,T]$. Our choice of
$C_Q$ gives $\|w_{\mathrm{app}}\|_{H^{s_h+1}}\le C_Q\majst\le\frac12$ there.

\emph{Continuity argument.} Let $t_*$ be the largest $t\in[0,T]$ such that
$\delta_{\mathrm r}(t)\le a/8$ and $\|w(\tau)\|_{H^{s_h}}\le1$ for $\tau\in[0,t]$; it
exists and is positive since $\delta_{\mathrm r}(0)=0$, $w(0)=0$, $\delta_{\mathrm r}$
is continuous (see its definition at the start of this section), and
$\|w\|_{H^{s_h}}$ is continuous by Theorem~\ref{thm:smooth}\textup{(1)}. Because $\supp\Phi\subset\{a\le|y|\le1\}$ and
$1+\frac a8\le\frac54$, the support of $\omega_{\mathrm r}(\tau)$ lies in
$\NNm\subset N$ for $\tau\le t_*$. So Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}\textup{(3)} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:energy} with $s=s_h$ apply on $[0,t_*]$; the conditions
$\|w_{\mathrm{app}}\|_{H^{s_h+1}}\le1$ and $\|h\|_{C^{s_h+1}(N')}\le\eta\le1$ hold.
Since $v=w-w_{\mathrm{app}}$ has $v(0)=0$, Equation~\eqref{eq:Q-res} gives, for
$t\le t_*$,
\[
\|v(t)\|_{H^{s_h}}\lesssim\int_0^te^{\Lam(s_h)(t-\tau)}\maj(\tau)^{N_Q}\big(\maj(\tau)+\eta(\tau)\big)\dd\tau .
\]
For $0\le\tau\le t$, (M1) and $N_Q\sigma\ge N_Q\sigb\ge\Lam(s_h)$ give
$e^{\Lam(s_h)(t-\tau)}\maj(\tau)^{N_Q}\le e^{(\Lam(s_h)-N_Q\sigma)(t-\tau)}\maj(t)^{N_Q}$,
which is at most $\maj(t)^{N_Q}$. By (M2) and (M3) we conclude, for $t\le t_*$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Q-v}
\|v(t)\|_{H^{s_h}}\lesssim\maj(t)^{N_Q}\int_0^t(\maj+\eta)\lesssim\big(1+\sigb^{-1}\big)\maj(t)^{N_Q+1}.
\end{equation}
Since $w=\wlin+\sum_{j\ge2}w^{[j]}+v$ and $\maj\le1$, Equations~\eqref{eq:Q-hier}
and \eqref{eq:Q-v} give, for $t\le t_*$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Q-b}
\|w(t)-\wlin(t)\|_{H^{s_h}}\le C_Q\maj(t)^2,\qquad\|w(t)\|_{H^{s_h}}\le C_Q\maj(t)\le\tfrac12 .
\end{equation}
For $y\in\supp\Phi$ and $0\le u\le t\le t_*$, the point $\flow_u(y)$ lies in $N$.
The background velocity $\Omega(|x|;G)x^\perp$ is tangential, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}\textup{(2)} gives
\[
\frac{\dd}{\dd u}|\flow_u(y)|
=\big(h+\KBS\ast w\big)(u,\flow_u(y))\cdot\er(\flow_u(y)).
\]
Since $\er$ is a unit vector and $\flow_u(y)\in N\subset B_3$, the absolute
value of the right side is at most
$\|h(u)\|_{C^0(N')}+C_{\mathrm{BS}}\|w(u)\|_{H^1}
\le\eta(u)+C_{\mathrm{BS}}\|w(u)\|_{H^{s_h}}$,
by Lemma~\ref{lem:bilinear}\textup{(4)} and $s_h\ge3$.
Integrating from $0$ to $\tau\le t$ and enlarging the integral to $[0,t]$
gives the first inequality below; Equation~\eqref{eq:Q-b}, (M2) and (M3)
then give the remaining bounds:
\[
\big||\flow_\tau(y)|-|y|\big|\le\int_0^t\big(\eta+C_{\mathrm{BS}}\|w\|_{H^{s_h}}\big)\le\maj(t)+\frac{C_{\mathrm{BS}}C_Q}{\sigb}\maj(t).
\]
So $\delta_{\mathrm r}\le(1+C_{\mathrm{BS}}C_Q/\sigb)\maj\le a/16$ and $\|w\|_{H^{s_h}}\le\frac12$ on
$[0,t_*]$. If $t_*<T$, continuity would give a larger $t$ with
$\delta_{\mathrm r}(t)\le a/8$ and $\|w\|_{H^{s_h}}\le1$ on $[0,t]$; hence $t_*=T$.
Then (a) follows from $\delta_{\mathrm r}\le(1+C_{\mathrm{BS}}C_Q/\sigb)\maj$ and Lemma~\ref{lem:annuli},
and (b), (c) are
Equation~\eqref{eq:Q-b}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{An Active Ring}

A ring is active once its deviation from $\Phi$ has a component along the
unstable eigenfunction that grows at the rate $\sigma(G)$. Theorem~\ref{thm:active}
shows that the deviation then follows this component, up to a transient and
controlled errors, until its envelope $\kappa e^{\sigma(G)t}$ reaches a small
size $\delta_0$. The transient reflects the slower linear evolution outside
the unstable eigenspace: its contribution becomes small relative to the
growing unstable component.
Corollary~\ref{cor:active-moment} gives the outer moment of the ring, through
which it drives the rings outside it. The unstable component of the linear
response is explicit, by Proposition~I.\ref{I-prop:semigroup}; the next lemma
writes it with a running coefficient $c(t)$, which accounts for the driving by
the velocity field $h$.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:running}
Let $G\in[0,\Gone]$, $\bar t>0$, $w_0\in X^0$ real, and let $h$ be continuous on
$[0,\bar t]$ with values in $C^0(N';\R^2)$ and $3$-fold rotationally symmetric. Put
$F(t)=-h(t)\cdot\nabla\Phi$ and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:running}
c(t)=\ellst(w_0)+\int_0^te^{-\lamst v}\,\ellst(F(v))\dd v\qquad(0\le t\le\bar t),
\end{equation}
the \textbf{running coefficient}. Then
$\wlin_0(t):=e^{t\Lin(G)}w_0+\int_0^te^{(t-v)\Lin(G)}F(v)\dd v$ satisfies
$\ellst(\wlin_0(t))=c(t)e^{\lamst t}$ and
$\Riesz\,\wlin_0(t)=2\Re\big(c(t)e^{\lamst t}\phist\big)$ for $0\le t\le\bar t$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The function $F$ is continuous on $[0,\bar t]$ with values in $X^0$, since
$\|h(t)\cdot\nabla\Phi-h(t')\cdot\nabla\Phi\|_{L^2}\le\|h(t)-h(t')\|_{C^0(N')}\|\nabla\Phi\|_{L^2}$,
and $F(t)$ is real, supported in $N$ and $3$-fold rotationally symmetric. The functional
$\ellst$ is bounded on $X^0$, so it commutes with the integral, and
Proposition~I.\ref{I-prop:semigroup} gives $\ellst(e^{\tau\Lin}f)=e^{\lamst\tau}\ellst(f)$
for $f\in X^0$. Hence
$\ellst(\wlin_0(t))=e^{\lamst t}\ellst(w_0)+\int_0^te^{\lamst(t-v)}\ellst(F(v))\dd v=c(t)e^{\lamst t}$.
Since $\wlin_0(t)$ is real, Definition~I.\ref{I-def:eigendata} gives
$\Riesz\,\wlin_0(t)=2\Re\big(\ellst(\wlin_0(t))\phist\big)=2\Re\big(c(t)e^{\lamst t}\phist\big)$.
\end{proof}

Recall that the Japanese bracket is $\jap{t}=(1+t^2)^{1/2}$, so
$\jap{t}^p$ is a polynomial weight that is nonzero at $t=0$.
On the range of $1-\Riesz$ the semigroup grows at most at a rate
$\sigma'<\sigb\le\sigma(G)$ (Proposition~I.\ref{I-prop:semigroup}).
The next lemma bounds this part of the linear response while keeping its
two sources of growth separate: the initial deviation and the polynomial
part of $h$ give a bound proportional to $\jap{t}^pe^{\sigma't}$, whereas
the part of $h$ growing like $e^{\sigma t}$ produces a response at that same
rate. The former becomes small relative to the growing unstable component;
the latter is controlled later by choosing its prefactor $\eta_b$ small.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:linear-part}
Assume \textup{(N1)}--\textup{(N3)}. Let $\sigma'\in(\frac9{200},\sigb)$, and let
$p\ge0$ and $k\ge2$ be integers. There is a constant $C_{\mathrm{lin}}(k)\ge1$,
depending only on $\Phi$, $\Gone$, $k$ and $\sigma'$, with the following property. Let $G\in[0,\Gone]$,
$\sigma=\sigma(G)$, $\lambda=\lamst(G)$, $\bar t>0$, $A\ge0$, $\kappa\ge0$ and
$\eta_b\ge0$. Let $w_0\in X^k$ be real with $\|w_0\|_{H^k}\le A$, and let $h$ be
continuous on $[0,\bar t]$ with values in $C^k(N';\R^2)$, $3$-fold rotationally symmetric, with
$\|h(t)\|_{C^k(N')}\le A\jap{t}^p+\eta_b\kappa e^{\sigma t}$. Let $c$ and $\wlin_0$ be as
in Lemma~\ref{lem:running}. Then for $t\in[0,\bar t]$,
\[
\begin{gathered}
\wlin_0(t)=2\Re\big(c(t)e^{\lambda t}\phist\big)+(1-\Riesz)\wlin_0(t),\\
\|(1-\Riesz)\wlin_0(t)\|_{H^k}\le C_{\mathrm{lin}}(k)\big(A\jap{t}^pe^{\sigma't}+\eta_b\kappa e^{\sigma t}\big).
\end{gathered}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The decomposition is Lemma~\ref{lem:running}. For real $f\in X^k$,
Proposition~I.\ref{I-prop:semigroup} gives
$\Riesz e^{\tau\Lin}f=2\Re\big(\ellst(e^{\tau\Lin}f)\phist\big)=2\Re\big(e^{\lambda\tau}\ellst(f)\phist\big)=e^{\tau\Lin}\Riesz f$.
Since $\Riesz$ is bounded on $X^k$, it commutes with the integral, and
$(1-\Riesz)\wlin_0(t)=e^{t\Lin}(1-\Riesz)w_0+\int_0^te^{(t-v)\Lin}(1-\Riesz)F(v)\dd v$.
By Proposition~I.\ref{I-prop:semigroup} and the bound
$\|h\cdot\nabla\Phi\|_{H^k}\le\Cnab(k)\|h\|_{C^k(N')}$ of Lemma~I.\ref{I-lem:uniform}, its
norm in $H^k$ is at most
$C_S(k,\sigma')\big(e^{\sigma't}A+\Cnab(k)\int_0^te^{\sigma'(t-v)}(A\jap{v}^p+\eta_b\kappa e^{\sigma v})\dd v\big)$.
Finally
$\int_0^te^{\sigma'(t-v)}\jap{v}^p\dd v\le\jap{t}^pe^{\sigma't}/\sigma'$ and
$\int_0^te^{\sigma'(t-v)}e^{\sigma v}\dd v\le e^{\sigma t}/(\sigma-\sigma')\le e^{\sigma t}/(\sigb-\sigma')$.
\end{proof}

For $x\in(0,1]$ we write $\varsigma(x)=1+\sigb^{-1}\log(1/x)$. Since
$\sigma(G)\ge\sigb$, this number bounds $1+t$ for $t\le\sigma(G)^{-1}\log(1/x)$,
the time in which a coefficient of size $x$ that grows at the rate $\sigma(G)$
reaches size $1$. The cubic time weight in the active-ring estimates below matches the
mode-$3$ moment bound for the inner vorticity: this moment is bounded by a
constant times the cube of its confinement radius, which grows at most linearly in time
(Proposition~\ref{prop:confine}). For an integer $s\ge3$ and a number
$\sigma'\in(\frac9{200},\sigb)$ we put
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:W-const}
N_G=\min\big\{n\in\Z:n\ge2,\ n\ge\Lam(s)/\sigb+1\big\},\qquad
M=s+N_G+2,\qquad N_W=5N_G+1.
\end{equation}
The semigroup bounds, Lemmas~\ref{lem:bilinear} and~\ref{lem:linear-part}, and
the eigenfunction bounds of Lemma~I.\ref{I-lem:uniform} have constants
uniform in $G$ through these derivative orders. Also
$\sup_{t\ge0}\jap{t}^3e^{-(\sigb-\sigma')t}<\infty$ because
$\sigb>\sigma'$. These bounds depend only on $(\Phi,\Gone,s,\sigma')$. As in
Theorem~\ref{thm:quiet}, the order $N_G$ of the approximate solution is chosen
large, so that its residual, which is one order higher, is small enough on the
window of Lemma~\ref{lem:window} to absorb the growth $e^{\Lam(s)t}$ allowed by
Lemma~\ref{lem:energy} (Equation~\eqref{eq:W-exp} below).

Lemma~\ref{lem:window} is the main step in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:active}.
It treats an active ring whose initial deviation and running coefficient are
bounded by a fixed multiple $C_\natural$ of a number $\kappa_1$, and whose field is
bounded accordingly. It does so on the window of time in which a component of
initial size $\kappa_1$, growing at the rate $\sigma(G)$, stays below $\delta_0$.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:window}
Assume \textup{(N1)}--\textup{(N3)}. Let $s\ge3$ be an integer and
$\sigma'\in(\frac9{200},\sigb)$, let $N_G$, $M$ and $N_W$ be as in
Equation~\eqref{eq:W-const}, and let $C_\natural\ge1$. There are $\delta_W\in(0,\frac12]$, $\kappa_W\in(0,1]$ and
$C_W\ge1$, depending only on $(\Phi,\Gone,s,\sigma',C_\natural)$, such that the
following holds. Let $G\in[0,\Gone]$, $\sigma=\sigma(G)$, $\lambda=\lamst(G)$,
$\kappa_1\in(0,\kappa_W]$, $\delta_0\in(0,\delta_W]$, $\eta_b\in[0,1]$,
$\hat T_1=\sigma^{-1}\log(\delta_0/\kappa_1)$ and $\bar t\in(0,\hat T_1]$. Let
$\omega$, $\omega_{\mathrm r},\omega_{\mathrm{inn}},\omega_{\mathrm{ou}}$ and $h$ be as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction} on $[0,\bar t]$ with this $G$, put
$w=\omega_{\mathrm r}-\Phi$, and let $c$ be the running coefficient of Equation~\eqref{eq:running}
with $w_0=w(0)$. Assume that $\|w(0)\|_{H^{M-2}}\le C_\natural\kappa_1$, that
$\supp(\Phi+w(0))\subset\{(\frac78-\frac1{64})a\le|y|\le\frac54+\frac a{64}\}$, and
that for $t\in[0,\bar t]$,
\[
\|h(t)\|_{C^M(N')}\le C_\natural\kappa_1\jap{t}^3+\eta_b\kappa_1e^{\sigma t},\qquad
|c(t)|\le C_\natural\kappa_1 .
\]
Put $\hat a_1(t)=\kappa_1e^{\sigma t}$ and $\rho_1(t)=\jap{t}^3e^{-(\sigma-\sigma')t}$.
Then for every $t\in[0,\bar t]$, every trajectory of $\omega_{\mathrm r}$ moves
radially by at most $C_W\delta_0+C_W\kappa_1\varsigma(\kappa_1)^{N_W}\le a/32$ on
$[0,t]$, and
\[
\big\|w(t)-2\Re\big(c(t)e^{\lambda t}\phist\big)\big\|_{H^s}\le C_W\hat a_1(t)\big[\rho_1(t)+\hat a_1(t)+\kappa_1\varsigma(\kappa_1)^{N_W}+\eta_b\big].
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $\Lam=\Lam(s)$ and $\Lin=\Lin(G)$. Since $N_G\ge\Lam/\sigb+1$ and
$\Lam\ge2\sigma\ge2\sigb$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:energy}),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:W-exp}
(N_G+1)\sigma-\Lam\ge2\sigma,\qquad\Lam-\sigma\ge\sigma\ge\sigb,\qquad N_G+1-\Lam/\sigma\ge2 .
\end{equation}

\emph{Constants.} All implicit constants in this proof depend only on
$(\Phi,\Gone,s,\sigma',C_\natural)$. The finite hierarchy, residual and energy
estimates below produce constants with these dependencies. Choose
$C_W\ge C_\natural$ large enough to dominate their coefficients and those in
the final norm and displacement estimates. Then choose
$\delta_W\in(0,1/2]$ so that $C_W\delta_W\le a/64$, and
$\kappa_W\in(0,1]$ so that
$C_Wx\varsigma(x)^{N_W}\le a/64$ for every $0<x\le\kappa_W$.
To justify the latter choice, recall that $N_W=5N_G+1$ and $\sigb>0$ are
already fixed. With $L=\log(1/x)$,
\[
x\varsigma(x)^{N_W}=e^{-L}(1+L/\sigb)^{N_W}.
\]
Its logarithmic derivative with respect to $L$ is
\[
-1+\frac{N_W}{\sigb+L}\le-\frac12
\qquad\text{whenever }\sigb+L\ge2N_W.
\]
Thus the quantity decreases to zero as $L\to\infty$. Choose $\kappa_W$
small enough that $\sigb+\log(1/\kappa_W)\ge2N_W$ and
$C_W\kappa_W\varsigma(\kappa_W)^{N_W}\le a/64$; monotonicity then gives
the required inequality for every $0<x\le\kappa_W$.

Now fix the data, and put $\varepsilon_W=C_\natural\kappa_1$,
$\mathfrak b(t)=\hat a_1(t)+\varepsilon_W\jap{t}^{5}$ and $k_j=M-1-j$ for
$1\le j\le N_G$, so $k_1=M-2$, $k_{N_G}=s+1$, $k_j+1=k_{j-1}$. The function
$\mathfrak b$ plays the role of $\maj$ in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:quiet}: the
terms $w^{[j]}$ below are bounded by multiples of $\hat a_1\mathfrak b^{j-1}$. For
$t\le\bar t\le\hat T_1\le\sigma^{-1}\log(1/\kappa_1)$ we have $\jap{t}\le1+t\le\varsigma(\kappa_1)$,
$\hat a_1(t)\le\delta_0$ and $\kappa_1\le\hat a_1(t)$. By the choice of $\kappa_W$ and
$\delta_W$, for $t\in[0,\bar t]$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:W-small}
\mathfrak b(t)\le\delta_0+C_\natural\kappa_1\varsigma(\kappa_1)^{5}\le1,\qquad
\|h(t)\|_{C^{s+1}(N')}\le C_\natural\kappa_1\varsigma(\kappa_1)^3+\delta_0\le1 .
\end{equation}

\emph{Approximate solution.} Let $w^{[j]}$ be the functions of
Equation~\eqref{eq:hier} with $J=N_G$ and $w^{[1]}=\wlin_0$, the function of
Lemma~\ref{lem:running} with $w_0=w(0)$. This choice of $w^{[1]}$ is admissible in
Equation~\eqref{eq:hier} because $\supp(\Phi+w(0))\subset N$, and the functions
$w^{[j]}$ are regular by Lemmas~\ref{lem:duhamel} and \ref{lem:bilinear}. We
claim that
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:W-hier}
\|w^{[j]}(t)\|_{H^{k_j}}\lesssim\hat a_1(t)\,\mathfrak b(t)^{j-1}\qquad(t\in[0,\bar t],\ 1\le j\le N_G).
\end{equation}
For $j=1$, Lemma~\ref{lem:linear-part} with $k=M-2$, $A=C_\natural\kappa_1$ and
$\kappa=\kappa_1$ gives
$\|(1-\Riesz)w^{[1]}(t)\|_{H^{M-2}}\lesssim\hat a_1(t)\big(C_\natural\rho_1(t)+\eta_b\big)$,
since $C_\natural\kappa_1\jap{t}^3e^{\sigma't}=C_\natural\hat a_1(t)\rho_1(t)$; and
$\|2\Re(c(t)e^{\lambda t}\phist)\|_{H^{M-2}}\lesssim C_\natural\hat a_1(t)$. As
$\rho_1$ is uniformly bounded and $\eta_b\le1$, this is Equation~\eqref{eq:W-hier} for $j=1$.
Let $j\ge2$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:bilinear}, Equation~\eqref{eq:W-hier} for the
indices less than $j$, and
$\|h(v)\|_{C^{k_j}(N')}\le\varepsilon_W\jap{v}^3+\hat a_1(v)$,
\[
\|g^{[j]}(v)\|_{H^{k_j}}\lesssim\hat a_1(v)\big(\hat a_1(v)+\varepsilon_W\jap{v}^3\big)\mathfrak b(v)^{j-2}.
\]
Since $e^{\sigma(t-v)}\hat a_1(v)=\hat a_1(t)$ and
$\|e^{(t-v)\Lin}\|\lesssim e^{\sigma(t-v)}$, it suffices to show that
\[
\int_0^t\big(\hat a_1+\varepsilon_W\jap{v}^3\big)\mathfrak b^{j-2}\dd v\le2^{j-1}\max(1,\sigb^{-1})\,\mathfrak b(t)^{j-1}.
\]
Expand $\mathfrak b^{j-2}=\sum_{i+q=j-2}\binom{j-2}i\hat a_1^i(\varepsilon_W\jap{v}^{5})^q$,
and use that $\hat a_1$ and $\jap{\cdot}$ are nondecreasing. Then
\[
\begin{gathered}
\int_0^t\hat a_1^{i+1}(\varepsilon_W\jap{v}^{5})^q\dd v\le\frac{\hat a_1(t)^{i+1}(\varepsilon_W\jap{t}^{5})^q}{(i+1)\sigma},\\
\int_0^t\varepsilon_W\jap{v}^3\hat a_1^i(\varepsilon_W\jap{v}^{5})^q\dd v\le\frac{\hat a_1(t)^i(\varepsilon_W\jap{t}^{5})^{q+1}}{i\sigma}\quad(i\ge1),\\
\int_0^t\varepsilon_W^{j-1}\jap{v}^{3+5(j-2)}\dd v\le(\varepsilon_W\jap{t}^{5})^{j-1},
\end{gathered}
\]
where the second line uses $\jap{t}^3\le\jap{t}^{5}$ and the third uses
$t\le\jap{t}$. Each right side is a monomial of degree $j-1$ in
$\hat a_1(t)$ and $\varepsilon_W\jap{t}^{5}$, hence at most
$\max(1,\sigb^{-1})\mathfrak b(t)^{j-1}$, and there are $2\cdot2^{j-2}$ terms counted with
the binomial weights. This proves Equation~\eqref{eq:W-hier}.

The residual is given by Equation~\eqref{eq:hier-res} with $J=N_G$. Since
$\mathfrak b\le1$ and $\hat a_1\le\mathfrak b$, we have
$\hat a_1^2\mathfrak b^{i+l-2}\le\hat a_1\mathfrak b^{N_G}$ for $i+l>N_G$, and
$\|h\|_{C^s(N')}\le\varepsilon_W\jap{t}^3+\hat a_1\le2\mathfrak b$. So
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:W-res}
\|R_{\mathrm{app}}(t)\|_{H^s}\lesssim\hat a_1(t)\mathfrak b(t)^{N_G},\qquad
\|w_{\mathrm{app}}(t)\|_{H^{s+1}}\lesssim\hat a_1(t)\le\delta_0 .
\end{equation}
Our choice of $C_W$ gives $\|w_{\mathrm{app}}\|_{H^{s+1}}\le C_W\delta_0\le1$.

\emph{Continuity argument.} Let $t_*$ be the largest $t\in[0,\bar t]$ with
$\delta_{\mathrm r}(t)\le a/16$ and $\|w(v)\|_{H^s}\le1$ for $v\in[0,t]$; at $t=0$
both hold, since $\delta_{\mathrm r}(0)=0$ and, as $s\le M-2$,
$\|w(0)\|_{H^s}\le C_\natural\kappa_1\le a/64$ (recall $C_W\ge C_\natural$, $\varsigma\ge1$
and the choice of $\kappa_W$). For $t\le t_*$ the
support of $\omega_{\mathrm r}(t)$ lies in
$\{\frac{51}{64}a\le|y|\le\frac54+\frac5{64}a\}\subset\Nflat\subset N$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:annuli}). So
Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}\textup{(3)} and Lemma~\ref{lem:energy} apply on $[0,t_*]$,
by Equations~\eqref{eq:W-small} and \eqref{eq:W-res}. As $v=w-w_{\mathrm{app}}$
vanishes at $t=0$, and
$\mathfrak b^{N_G}\le2^{N_G-1}\big(\hat a_1^{N_G}+(\varepsilon_W\jap{\cdot}^{5})^{N_G}\big)$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|v(t)\|_{H^s}\lesssim\Big(&\int_0^te^{\Lam(t-v)}\hat a_1(v)^{N_G+1}\dd v\\
&+(\varepsilon_W\jap{t}^{5})^{N_G}\int_0^te^{\Lam(t-v)}\hat a_1(v)\dd v\Big).
\end{aligned}
\]
By Equation~\eqref{eq:W-exp}, the first integral is at most
$\hat a_1(t)^{N_G+1}/((N_G+1)\sigma-\Lam)\le\hat a_1(t)^{N_G+1}/(2\sigb)$, and the second is
at most $\kappa_1e^{\Lam t}/(\Lam-\sigma)\le\kappa_1e^{\Lam t}/\sigb$. For $t\le\hat T_1$,
$\kappa_1e^{\Lam t}\le\kappa_1^{1-\Lam/\sigma}\delta_0^{\Lam/\sigma}$. Hence, for $t\le t_*$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:W-v}
\|v(t)\|_{H^s}\lesssim\big(\hat a_1(t)^{N_G+1}+\Theta_W(t)\big),\qquad
\Theta_W(t)=(\varepsilon_W\jap{t}^{5})^{N_G}\kappa_1^{1-\Lam/\sigma}\delta_0^{\Lam/\sigma},
\end{equation}
and, by Equation~\eqref{eq:W-exp}, $\kappa_1\le1$ and $\delta_0\le1$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:W-theta}
\Theta_W(t)\le C_\natural^{N_G}\varsigma(\kappa_1)^{5N_G}\kappa_1^2\le C_\natural^{N_G}\kappa_1\varsigma(\kappa_1)^{N_W}\cdot\kappa_1 .
\end{equation}

We collect the bounds for $t\le t_*$. The leading approximation is the
unstable part of the linear response $w^{[1]}=\wlin_0$:
Lemma~\ref{lem:running} gives $\Riesz w^{[1]}=2\Re(ce^{\lambda t}\phist)$.
Splitting $w^{[1]}=\Riesz w^{[1]}+(1-\Riesz)w^{[1]}$ in
$w=\sum_{j=1}^{N_G}w^{[j]}+v$ therefore yields
\[
w-2\Re(ce^{\lambda t}\phist)
=(1-\Riesz)w^{[1]}+\sum_{j=2}^{N_G}w^{[j]}+v.
\]
The higher-order terms $w^{[j]}$, $j\ge2$, retain both their unstable and
complementary components; their full $H^s$ norms are already small by
Equation~\eqref{eq:W-hier}, so both components are included in the error.
More precisely, $\mathfrak b\le1$ gives $\mathfrak b^{j-1}\le\mathfrak b$ for
$j\ge2$, so their sum is bounded by a constant times $\hat a_1\mathfrak b$.
Substituting $\mathfrak b=\hat a_1+C_\natural\kappa_1\jap{t}^5$ and
$\jap{t}\le\varsigma(\kappa_1)$ produces the term
$C_\natural\kappa_1\varsigma(\kappa_1)^5$ after factoring out $\hat a_1$.
By the bound on
$(1-\Riesz)w^{[1]}$ above, Equation~\eqref{eq:W-hier} with $\mathfrak b\le1$,
Equations~\eqref{eq:W-v} and \eqref{eq:W-theta}, $\hat a_1^{N_G+1}\le\hat a_1^2$ and $\Theta_W\le\hat a_1\Theta_W/\kappa_1$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\big\|w-2\Re(ce^{\lambda t}\phist)\big\|_{H^s}\lesssim{}&\hat a_1(C_\natural\rho_1+\eta_b)\\
&+\hat a_1\big(2\hat a_1+C_\natural\kappa_1\varsigma(\kappa_1)^{5}+C_\natural^{N_G}\kappa_1\varsigma(\kappa_1)^{N_W}\big),
\end{aligned}
\]
which, after including the implicit constant in the choice of $C_W$, is at most
$C_W\hat a_1\big[\rho_1+\hat a_1+\kappa_1\varsigma(\kappa_1)^{N_W}+\eta_b\big]$.
Similarly,
$\|w(t)\|_{H^s}\le C\big(\hat a_1(t)+\Theta_W(t)\big)\le C_W\big(\delta_0+\kappa_1\varsigma(\kappa_1)^{N_W}\big)\le\tfrac a{32}$.
For the displacement, Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}\textup{(2)} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:bilinear}\textup{(4)} give, for $t\le t_*$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\delta_{\mathrm r}(t)&\le\int_0^t\big(\|h\|_{C^0(N')}+C_{\mathrm{BS}}\|w\|_{H^s}\big)\\
&\lesssim C_\natural\kappa_1\varsigma(\kappa_1)^{4}+\frac{\delta_0}{\sigb}+C_{\mathrm{BS}}\Big(\frac{\delta_0}{\sigb}+C_\natural^{N_G}\varsigma(\kappa_1)^{N_W}\kappa_1^2\Big),
\end{aligned}
\]
where we used $\|w\|_{H^s}\le\sum_j\|w^{[j]}\|_{H^s}+\|v\|_{H^s}\lesssim\hat a_1+\Theta_W$,
$\int_0^t\hat a_1\le\hat a_1(t)/\sigma\le\delta_0/\sigb$, and
$\int_0^t\Theta_W\le\bar t\,C_\natural^{N_G}\varsigma(\kappa_1)^{5N_G}\kappa_1^2$ with
$\bar t\le\varsigma(\kappa_1)$. After including the implicit constant, our choice
of $C_W$ bounds the right side by
$C_W\delta_0+C_W\kappa_1\varsigma(\kappa_1)^{N_W}\le a/32$. If $t_*<\bar t$, continuity
would give a larger $t$ with $\delta_{\mathrm r}(t)\le a/16$ and $\|w\|_{H^s}\le1$ on $[0,t]$, a
contradiction. So $t_*=\bar t$, and the bounds just proved hold on $[0,\bar t]$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:active}
Assume \textup{(N1)}--\textup{(N3)}. Fix an integer $s\ge3$ and a number
$\sigma'\in(\frac9{200},\sigb)$. Let $N_G$ be the least integer
with $N_G\ge2$ and $N_G\ge\Lam(s)/\sigb+1$, and put $M=s+N_G+2$ (so $M\ge s+4$).
There are $\dH\in(0,1)$, $C_2\ge1$ and $C_3>0$, depending only on
$(\Phi,\Gone,s,\sigma')$, and for every $C_0\ge1$ a number $\kappa_0(C_0)>0$,
depending only on $C_0$ and $(\Phi,\Gone,s,\sigma')$, such that the following holds.

Let $G\in[0,\Gone]$, $\sigma=\sigma(G)$, $\lambda=\lamst(G)$, $\kappa>0$,
$\eta_b\in[0,1]$, $\delta_0\in(0,\dH]$, $\hat a(t)=\kappa e^{\sigma t}$ and
$\hat T=\sigma^{-1}\log(\delta_0/\kappa)$. Let $t_1=t_1(C_0,G)$ be the least
$t\ge0$ such that $2^{3/2}C_0\jap{t'}^3e^{-(\sigma-\sigma')t'}\le1$ for all $t'\ge t$,
and define the \textbf{transient}
\[
\rhohat(t)=C_0\jap{t}^3e^{-(\sigma-\sigma')t}\ (0\le t<t_1),\qquad
\rhohat(t)=\jap{t-t_1}^3e^{-(\sigma-\sigma')(t-t_1)}\ (t\ge t_1).
\]
Let $\bar t>0$, let $\omega$, $\omega_{\mathrm r},\omega_{\mathrm{inn}},\omega_{\mathrm{ou}}$
and $h$ be as in Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction} on $[0,\bar t]$ with this $G$, put
$w(t)=\omega_{\mathrm r}(t)-\Phi$, and let $c$ be the running coefficient of
Equation~\eqref{eq:running} with $w_0=w(0)$. Assume:
\begin{description}[font=\normalfont]
\item[\textup{(Init)}] $\|w(0)\|_{H^{M-1}}\le C_0\kappa$ and $\supp(\Phi+w(0))\subset\NNm$;
\item[\textup{(Field)}] $h=h_1+h_2$ on $[0,\bar t]$ with
  $\|h_1(t)\|_{C^M(N')}\le C_0\kappa\jap{t}^3$ and
  $\|h_2(t)\|_{C^M(N')}\le\eta_b\,\hat a(t)$;
\item[\textup{(Coef)}] $|c(t)|\le\kappa\big(2+C_0\jap{t}^3e^{-\sigma t}\big)$ on $[0,\bar t]$;
\item[\textup{(Small)}] $\kappa\le\kappa_0(C_0)$.
\end{description}
Then for every $t\in[0,\min(\bar t,\hat T)]$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item $\supp(\Phi+w(t))\subset\NN$;
\item $\big\|w(t)-2\Re\big(c(t)e^{\lambda t}\phist\big)\big\|_{H^s}\le
  C_2\hat a(t)\big[\rhohat(t)+\hat a(t)+\kappa^{1/2}+\eta_b\big]$ and
  $\|w(t)\|_{H^s}\le C_2\hat a(t)(1+\rhohat(t))$.
\end{enumerate}
Moreover, let $\sigma_{\max}=\max_{G\in[0,\Gone]}\sigma(G)$, and let $\bar t_1(C_0)$ be the
least $t\ge0$ such that
\[
2^{3/2}C_0\jap{t'}^3e^{-(\sigb-\sigma')t'}\le1\qquad\text{for all }t'\ge t .
\]
Then $t_1(C_0,G)\le\bar t_1(C_0)$, and for all $t\ge0$,
\[
\hat a(t)\rhohat(t)\le C(C_0)\,\kappa\jap{t}^3e^{\sigma't},\qquad
C(C_0)=\max\big(C_0,e^{\sigma_{\max}\bar t_1(C_0)}\big);
\]
the number $C(C_0)$ depends only on $C_0$, $\sigma'$, $\sigb$ and $\sigma_{\max}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Throughout,
$\Lin=\Lin(G)$, $F=-h\cdot\nabla\Phi$, and $\wlin$ is the function $\wlin_0$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:running} with $w_0=w(0)$; it is an admissible choice of $w^{[1]}$
in Equation~\eqref{eq:hier}, because $\supp(\Phi+w(0))\subset\NNm\subset N$ by
(Init). The proof has two stages. We call $[0,\min(\bar t,t_1)]$ the
\emph{initial layer}: during this initial interval, the contribution of the
initial data and polynomial driving may still be large relative to
$\kappa e^{\sigma t}$. By the definition of $t_1$, the factor
$C_0\jap{t}^3e^{-(\sigma-\sigma')t}$ controlling this relative size is at most
$2^{-3/2}$ for every $t\ge t_1$.
The initial interval has length at most $\bar t_1(C_0)$, independently of
$\kappa$, and we control it by comparing $w$ with $\wlin$ using
Lemma~\ref{lem:energy}. At $t_1$ the relative contribution is small enough
to restart the problem and apply Lemma~\ref{lem:window} on the remaining interval.

\emph{Constants.} Fix a constant $C\ge1$, depending only on
$(\Phi,\Gone,s,\sigma')$, that bounds the semigroup, eigenfunction and
linear-response estimates used below. Let $\|\ellst\|$ and $\Cp$ be as in
Lemma~I.\ref{I-lem:uniform}. Choose $C_\natural\ge4$ sufficiently large for
the restarted initial-data bound below, in particular $C_\natural\ge8C+1$.
Let $\delta_W,\kappa_W,C_W$ be the constants of Lemma~\ref{lem:window} for
this $C_\natural$, and choose
\[
C_2\ge1,\qquad C_3\ge(1+\Cp)C_2,\qquad
0<\dH\le\delta_W,\quad C_2\dH\le a/32,
\]
with $C_2$ large enough for the norm and displacement estimates below.
These depend only on $(\Phi,\Gone,s,\sigma')$. Now let $C_0\ge1$, write
$\bar t_1=\bar t_1(C_0)$, $\Lambda'=\Lam(M-1)$ and $\Lambda''=\Lam(M-2)$.
On the fixed interval $[0,\bar t_1]$, the estimates below bound
$\|h\|_{C^M}/\kappa$, $\|w\|_{H^{M-1}}/\kappa$,
$\delta_{\mathrm r}/\kappa$, $\|\wlin\|_{H^{M-1}}/\kappa$ and
$\|w-\wlin\|_{H^{M-2}}/\kappa^2$ by constants depending only on $C_0$ and
$(\Phi,\Gone,s,\sigma')$. The implicit constants in these initial-layer
estimates may also depend on $C_0$ and are obtained independently of the
constant now chosen. Choose one fixed $C_{\mathrm{lay}}=C_{\mathrm{lay}}(C_0)\ge C_0+1$ large enough to
dominate all these coefficients.
The smallness of $\kappa$ is imposed by three groups of conditions: (L1) is used on
the initial layer, (L2) to bound the terms that contain the error $v_1=w-\wlin$ of
the initial layer, and (L3) to apply Lemma~\ref{lem:window} after the restart.
Choose $\kappa_0(C_0)\in(0,(a/32)^2]$ sufficiently small that every
$\kappa\in(0,\kappa_0(C_0)]$ satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(L\arabic*)}]
\item $C_{\mathrm{lay}}\kappa\le\min(\frac12,\frac a{128},\frac12\kappa^{1/2})$;
\item $(1+\|\ellst\|)C_{\mathrm{lay}}\kappa\le1$ and
  $4(1+C\|\ellst\|)C_{\mathrm{lay}}\kappa^{1/2}\le1$;
\item $e^{\sigma_{\max}\bar t_1}\kappa\le\kappa_W$ and
  $C_We^{\sigma_{\max}\bar t_1}\kappa\,\varsigma(\kappa)^{N_W}\le\frac12\kappa^{1/2}$.
\end{enumerate}
Such a choice is possible because in each inequality the ratio of the left
side to the right side tends to zero as $\kappa\to0^+$. Thus $\kappa_0(C_0)$
depends only on $C_0$ and $(\Phi,\Gone,s,\sigma')$, and (Small) gives
(L1)--(L3) and $\kappa\le(a/32)^2$.

\emph{The time $t_1$.} Since $\sigma\ge\sigb$,
$2^{3/2}C_0\jap{t'}^3e^{-(\sigma-\sigma')t'}\le2^{3/2}C_0\jap{t'}^3e^{-(\sigb-\sigma')t'}$, so
$t_1\le\bar t_1$. For $t\ge t_1$, the definition of $t_1$ with $t'=t$ gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:A-t1}
C_0\jap{t}^3e^{-\sigma t}\le2^{-3/2}e^{-\sigma't}\le1,\qquad\text{hence }|c(t)|\le3\kappa\text{ by (Coef)}.
\end{equation}
For $t<t_1$,
$\hat a(t)\rhohat(t)=C_0\kappa\jap{t}^3e^{\sigma't}$. For $t\ge t_1$,
$\hat a(t)\rhohat(t)=\kappa e^{\sigma t_1}\jap{t-t_1}^3e^{\sigma'(t-t_1)}\le e^{\sigma_{\max}\bar t_1}\kappa\jap{t}^3e^{\sigma't}$.
This proves the last assertion of the theorem.

\emph{The initial layer $[0,t_{\mathrm{in}}]$, $t_{\mathrm{in}}=\min(\bar t,t_1)$.}
Here $t\le t_1\le\bar t_1$, $\sigma\le\sigma_{\max}$ and $\eta_b\le1$.
Thus the triangle inequality and (Field) give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|h(t)\|_{C^M(N')}
&\le C_0\kappa\jap{t}^3+\eta_b\kappa e^{\sigma t}\\
&\le\kappa\big(C_0\jap{t}^3+e^{\sigma_{\max}t}\big)
\le C_{\mathrm{lay}}\kappa\le1,
\end{aligned}
\]
by the choice of $C_{\mathrm{lay}}$ and (L1).

Let $t_*$ be the largest $t\in[0,t_{\mathrm{in}}]$ such that
$\delta_{\mathrm r}(t)\le a/64$ and $\|w(v)\|_{H^{M-1}}\le1$ for $v\in[0,t]$.
At $t=0$, $\delta_{\mathrm r}(0)=0$, and (Init) gives
$\|w(0)\|_{H^{M-1}}\le C_0\kappa<C_{\mathrm{lay}}\kappa\le1$:
indeed, $C_{\mathrm{lay}}\ge C_0+1$, and (L1) gives $C_{\mathrm{lay}}\kappa\le1/2<1$. Both continuity conditions therefore hold
strictly at $t=0$.
For $t\le t_*$ the support of $\omega_{\mathrm r}(t)$ lies in
$\{(\frac78-\frac1{64})a\le|y|\le\frac54+\frac a{64}\}\subset N$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:annuli}), so Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}\textup{(3)} applies on $[0,t_*]$.

We first obtain a bound of order $\kappa$ for $w$ itself. Taking
$w_{\mathrm{app}}=0$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:energy} makes its error equal to $w$
and its residual equal to $h\cdot\nabla\Phi$. At level $M-1$, the lemma's
hypotheses hold by the continuity assumption on $w$ and the bound on
$\|h\|_{C^M(N')}$ above. Lemma~\ref{lem:bilinear}\textup{(3)} gives
$\|R_{\mathrm{app}}\|_{H^{M-1}}\lesssim\kappa$, with a coefficient depending only on $C_0$ and the fixed data.
Consequently,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|w(t)\|_{H^{M-1}}
&\lesssim e^{\Lambda't}C_0\kappa
 +\kappa\int_0^te^{\Lambda'(t-v)}\dd v\\
&\lesssim e^{\Lambda'\bar t_1}(C_0+\bar t_1)\kappa
 \le C_{\mathrm{lay}}\kappa\le\tfrac12.
\end{aligned}
\]
The displayed coefficient depends only on $C_0$ and the fixed data and is
included in the choice of $C_{\mathrm{lay}}$; the last inequality is (L1).
Likewise, Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}\textup{(2)} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:bilinear}\textup{(4)} yield
\[
\delta_{\mathrm r}(t)
\le\int_0^t\big(\|h\|_{C^0(N')}+C_{\mathrm{BS}}\|w\|_{H^1}\big)
\lesssim\bar t_1\kappa\le C_{\mathrm{lay}}\kappa\le a/128.
\]
Here the coefficient follows from the bounds on $h$ and $w$ just proved,
and the last inequality is again (L1).
These strict improvements of both continuity bounds imply $t_*=t_{\mathrm{in}}$.

We next compare $w$ with its linear response. Lemma~\ref{lem:duhamel} gives
$\wlin\in C([0,\bar t];X^{M-1})\cap C^1([0,\bar t];X^{M-2})$, real-valued,
with $\partial_t\wlin=\Lin\wlin+F$ and $\wlin(0)=w(0)$.
In its Duhamel formula, Proposition~I.\ref{I-prop:semigroup} bounds the
semigroup by $Ce^{\sigma t}$ at level $M-1$, while
Lemma~\ref{lem:bilinear}\textup{(3)} gives
$\|F(v)\|_{H^{M-1}}\lesssim\kappa$ with the same allowed dependencies.
Thus, for $t\le t_{\mathrm{in}}$,
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\wlin(t)\|_{H^{M-1}}
&\lesssim e^{\sigma t}C_0\kappa
 +\kappa\int_0^te^{\sigma(t-v)}\dd v\\
&\lesssim e^{\sigma_{\max}\bar t_1}(C_0+\bar t_1)\kappa
 \le C_{\mathrm{lay}}\kappa\le1.
\end{aligned}
\]
The penultimate coefficient depends only on $C_0$ and the fixed data and is
included in the choice of $C_{\mathrm{lay}}$; the last inequality is (L1).
This bound supplies the $H^{M-1}$ hypothesis on the approximate solution
when applying Lemma~\ref{lem:energy} at level $M-2$ with
$w_{\mathrm{app}}=\wlin$. The required bounds on $w$ and $h$ follow from
those already proved, since $H^{M-1}\subset H^{M-2}$ and
$\|h\|_{C^{M-1}(N')}\le\|h\|_{C^M(N')}\le1$.

By Equation~\eqref{eq:hier-res} with $J=1$, the residual is
$R_{\mathrm{app}}=-B(\wlin,\wlin)+h\cdot\nabla\wlin$.
The bilinear estimates of Lemma~\ref{lem:bilinear} give
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|R_{\mathrm{app}}\|_{H^{M-2}}
&\lesssim
 \big(\|\wlin\|_{H^{M-2}}+\|h\|_{C^{M-2}(N')}\big)
 \|\wlin\|_{H^{M-1}}\\
&\lesssim\kappa^2.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here we use the preceding order-$\kappa$ bounds with their coefficients
depending only on $C_0$ and the fixed data, before bounding them by $C_{\mathrm{lay}}$.
Since $v_1=w-\wlin$ has $v_1(0)=0$, Lemma~\ref{lem:energy} now yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
\|v_1(t)\|_{H^{M-2}}
&\lesssim\kappa^2\int_0^te^{\Lambda''(t-v)}\dd v\\
&\lesssim\bar t_1e^{\Lambda''\bar t_1}\kappa^2
 \le C_{\mathrm{lay}}\kappa^2,
\end{aligned}
\]
where the constants in the preceding bounds depend only on $C_0$ and the
fixed data, and $C_{\mathrm{lay}}$ dominates the final coefficient.
Together with the radial-displacement bound proved above, this gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:A-layer}
\|v_1(t)\|_{H^{M-2}}\le C_{\mathrm{lay}}\kappa^2,\qquad \delta_{\mathrm r}(t)\le C_{\mathrm{lay}}\kappa\qquad(0\le t\le t_{\mathrm{in}}).
\end{equation}

\emph{Proof of (b) for $t\le\min(t_{\mathrm{in}},\hat T)$.} By
Lemma~\ref{lem:linear-part} with $k=s$ and $A=C_0\kappa$, and since
$C_0\kappa\jap{t}^3e^{\sigma't}\le\hat a(t)\rhohat(t)$ for $t\le t_1$ (with
equality for $t<t_1$, while at $t=t_1$ the left side is at most
$2^{-3/2}\hat a(t_1)$ by the definition of $t_1$ and $\rhohat(t_1)=1$),
\[
\begin{gathered}
\wlin(t)=2\Re\big(c(t)e^{\lambda t}\phist\big)+(1-\Riesz)\wlin(t),\\
\|(1-\Riesz)\wlin(t)\|_{H^s}\le C\hat a(t)\big(\rhohat(t)+\eta_b\big).
\end{gathered}
\]
By Equation~\eqref{eq:A-layer} and (L2),
$\|v_1(t)\|_{H^s}\le C_{\mathrm{lay}}\kappa^2\le\kappa^{1/2}\kappa\le\kappa^{1/2}\hat a(t)$. Hence
$\|w-2\Re(ce^{\lambda t}\phist)\|_{H^s}\le(C+1)\hat a\big[\rhohat+\kappa^{1/2}+\eta_b\big]$.
By (Coef), $|c(t)|e^{\sigma t}\le2\hat a(t)+C_0\kappa\jap{t}^3\le\hat a(t)(2+\rhohat(t))$,
so $\|w(t)\|_{H^s}\le2C(2+\rhohat)\hat a+(C+1)(\rhohat+2)\hat a\le C_2\hat a(1+\rhohat)$.
The displacement is at most $C_{\mathrm{lay}}\kappa\le\frac12\kappa^{1/2}$ by (L1). This
proves (b), and bounds the displacement by $\frac12\kappa^{1/2}$, when
$\min(\bar t,\hat T)\le t_1$.

\emph{Restart.} Assume now that $t_1<t_e:=\min(\bar t,\hat T)$. The function
$\tilde\omega(t')=\omega(t_1+t')$ is the solution of Theorem~\ref{thm:smooth} with the
$3$-fold rotationally symmetric datum $\omega(t_1)$, by Theorem~\ref{thm:smooth}\textup{(5)}, and its
flow is $\tilde\flow_{t'}=\flow_{t_1+t'}\circ\flow_{t_1}^{-1}$. With
$\tilde\omega_\bullet(0)=\omega_\bullet(t_1)$ we get $\tilde\omega_\bullet(t')=\omega_\bullet(t_1+t')$,
the same $G$ (since $\int\omega_{\mathrm{inn}}(t_1)=2\pi G$), and the field
$\tilde h(t')=h(t_1+t')$. So the restarted configuration satisfies the assumptions of
Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction} on $[0,\bar t-t_1]$, and its perturbation is
$\tilde w(t')=w(t_1+t')$. Its running coefficient is
$\tilde c(t')=\ellst(w(t_1))+\int_0^{t'}e^{-\lambda v}\ellst(F(t_1+v))\dd v$. By
Equation~\eqref{eq:running} and Lemma~\ref{lem:running},
$e^{\lambda t_1}c(t_1+t')=\ellst(\wlin(t_1))+\int_0^{t'}e^{-\lambda v}\ellst(F(t_1+v))\dd v$, so
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:A-restart}
\tilde c(t')=e^{\lambda t_1}c(t_1+t')+\ellst(v_1(t_1)).
\end{equation}

We check the assumptions of Lemma~\ref{lem:window} for the restarted problem,
with $C_\natural$ as above, $\kappa_1=\hat a(t_1)=\kappa e^{\sigma t_1}$, the same
$\delta_0$ and $\eta_b$, and $\bar t'=t_e-t_1$. First,
$\kappa\le\kappa_1\le e^{\sigma_{\max}\bar t_1}\kappa\le\kappa_W$ by (L3),
$\delta_0\le\dH\le\delta_W$, and $\sigma^{-1}\log(\delta_0/\kappa_1)=\hat T-t_1\ge\bar t'$.
For the initial norm at the restart, write
\[
w(t_1)=2\Re\big(c(t_1)e^{\lambda t_1}\phist\big)
 +(1-\Riesz)\wlin(t_1)+v_1(t_1).
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:A-t1} and (Coef) give $|c(t_1)|\le3\kappa$.
The definition of $t_1$ also gives
\[
C_0\kappa\jap{t_1}^3e^{\sigma't_1}
=\kappa_1 C_0\jap{t_1}^3e^{-(\sigma-\sigma')t_1}
\le2^{-3/2}\kappa_1\le\kappa_1,
\qquad \eta_b\kappa e^{\sigma t_1}\le\kappa_1.
\]
We bound the three terms separately. First,
$\|\phist\|_{H^{M-2}}\le C$, so
\[
\big\|2\Re\big(c(t_1)e^{\lambda t_1}\phist\big)\big\|_{H^{M-2}}
\le2C|c(t_1)|e^{\sigma t_1}\le6C\kappa_1.
\]
Next, Lemma~\ref{lem:linear-part} with $k=M-2$ and $A=C_0\kappa$ yields
\[
\|(1-\Riesz)\wlin(t_1)\|_{H^{M-2}}
\le C\big(C_0\kappa\jap{t_1}^3e^{\sigma't_1}
 +\eta_b\kappa e^{\sigma t_1}\big)
\le2C\kappa_1.
\]
Finally, in the restart case the initial interval ends at $t_1$, so
Equation~\eqref{eq:A-layer} applies there. The first inequality of (L2),
$(1+\|\ellst\|)C_{\mathrm{lay}}\kappa\le1$, implies
$C_{\mathrm{lay}}\kappa^2\le\kappa\le\kappa_1$. Thus
$\|v_1(t_1)\|_{H^{M-2}}\le\kappa_1$. Adding the three bounds gives
\[
\|w(t_1)\|_{H^{M-2}}
\le(8C+1)\kappa_1\le C_\natural\kappa_1,
\]
by our choice of $C_\natural$.
Second, by Equation~\eqref{eq:A-layer} the support of $\Phi+w(t_1)$ lies in
$\{(\frac78-\frac1{64})a\le|y|\le\frac54+\frac a{64}\}$. Third, by Peetre's inequality
$\jap{t_1+t'}\le\sqrt2\jap{t_1}\jap{t'}$ and the definition of $t_1$,
\[
C_0\kappa\jap{t_1+t'}^3\le\big[2^{3/2}C_0\jap{t_1}^3e^{-(\sigma-\sigma')t_1}\big]e^{-\sigma't_1}\kappa_1\jap{t'}^3\le\kappa_1\jap{t'}^3,
\]
and $\eta_b\hat a(t_1+t')=\eta_b\kappa_1e^{\sigma t'}$; so
$\|\tilde h(t')\|_{C^M(N')}\le C_\natural\kappa_1\jap{t'}^3+\eta_b\kappa_1e^{\sigma t'}$.
Fourth, for every $t'\in[0,\bar t']$, $t_1+t'\ge t_1$, so (Coef) and
Equation~\eqref{eq:A-t1} give
\[
|c(t_1+t')|\le\kappa\big(2+C_0\jap{t_1+t'}^3e^{-\sigma(t_1+t')}\big)
\le3\kappa.
\]
Since $\|\ellst\|$ bounds the operator norm on $X^0$,
Equation~\eqref{eq:A-layer} gives
\[
|\ellst(v_1(t_1))|
\le\|\ellst\|\,\|v_1(t_1)\|_{L^2}
\le\|\ellst\|C_{\mathrm{lay}}\kappa^2\le\kappa\le\kappa_1,
\]
where the penultimate inequality again follows from the first inequality
of (L2). Substituting these bounds into Equation~\eqref{eq:A-restart}, and
using $|e^{\lambda t_1}|=e^{\sigma t_1}$ and $C_\natural\ge4$, yields
\[
\begin{aligned}
|\tilde c(t')|
&\le e^{\sigma t_1}|c(t_1+t')|+|\ellst(v_1(t_1))|\\
&\le3\kappa e^{\sigma t_1}+\kappa_1
=4\kappa_1\le C_\natural\kappa_1.
\end{aligned}
\]

\emph{Proof of (b) for $t\in[t_1,t_e]$.} Lemma~\ref{lem:window} applies; put
$t'=t-t_1$. Then $\kappa_1e^{\sigma t'}=\hat a(t)$ and
$\jap{t'}^3e^{-(\sigma-\sigma')t'}=\rhohat(t)$. Since $\varsigma$ is decreasing and
$\kappa_1\ge\kappa$, (L3) gives $C_W\kappa_1\varsigma(\kappa_1)^{N_W}\le\frac12\kappa^{1/2}$.
By Equation~\eqref{eq:A-restart},
$\tilde c(t')e^{\lambda t'}=c(t)e^{\lambda t}+\ellst(v_1(t_1))e^{\lambda t'}$, and
$|\ellst(v_1(t_1))e^{\lambda t'}|\le\|\ellst\|C_{\mathrm{lay}}\kappa^2e^{\sigma t'}\le\|\ellst\|C_{\mathrm{lay}}\kappa\,\hat a(t)$.
Since $\|2\Re(X\phist)\|_{H^s}\le2C|X|$ for $X\in\C$, (L2) gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\big\|w(t)-2\Re\big(c(t)e^{\lambda t}\phist\big)\big\|_{H^s}
&\le C_W\hat a(t)\big[\rhohat(t)+\hat a(t)+\tfrac12\kappa^{1/2}+\eta_b\big]\\
&\quad+\tfrac12\kappa^{1/2}\hat a(t),
\end{aligned}
\]
which gives the first bound of (b), since $C_W+1\le C_2$. By
Equation~\eqref{eq:A-t1}, the main term $2\Re(c(t)e^{\lambda t}\phist)$ has $H^s$ norm
at most $6C\hat a(t)$. Since $\hat a+\kappa^{1/2}+\eta_b\le3$, we get
\[
\|w(t)\|_{H^s}\le6C\hat a+(C_W+1)\hat a(\rhohat+3)\le C_2\hat a(1+\rhohat).
\]
For the displacement, a trajectory of $\omega_{\mathrm r}$ satisfies, with $z=\flow_{t_1}(y)$,
\[
|\flow_t(y)|-|y|=\big(|\tilde\flow_{t'}(z)|-|z|\big)+\big(|\flow_{t_1}(y)|-|y|\big),
\]
so by Lemma~\ref{lem:window} and Equation~\eqref{eq:A-layer} it moves radially by at
most $C_W\delta_0+\frac12\kappa^{1/2}+C_{\mathrm{lay}}\kappa\le C_2\delta_0+\kappa^{1/2}$.

\emph{Proof of (a).} In both cases the displacement is at most
$C_2\delta_0+\kappa^{1/2}\le\frac a{32}+\frac a{32}=\frac a{16}$, because
$\delta_0\le\dH\le a/(32C_2)$ and $\kappa\le(a/32)^2$. Since
$\supp\omega_{\mathrm r}(0)\subset\NNm$ by (Init), Lemma~\ref{lem:annuli} gives
$\supp(\Phi+w(t))\subset\nobreak\NN$.
\end{proof}

Recall from the expansion after Definition~I.\ref{I-def:multipole} that the outer
moment $\pout_3$ of a function determines the part in the angular modes $\pm3$ of its
field outside its support. So the outer moment of the deviation of a ring
determines the part in these modes of the field that the ring exerts on the rings
outside it. Recall also from Definition~I.\ref{I-def:eigendata} and the discussion
before it that $\pout_*(G)=\pout_3[\phist(G)]$ is the outer moment of the unstable
eigenfunction.
The next corollary gives this moment for an active ring.

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:active-moment}
Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:active}, put
$q(t)=2\pi\,\pout_3[w(t)]$. Then for every $t\in[0,\min(\bar t,\hat T)]$,
\[
\big|q(t)-2\pi\pout_*(G)\,c(t)e^{\lambda t}\big|\le2\pi C_3\,\hat a(t)\big[\rhohat(t)+\hat a(t)+\kappa^{1/2}+\eta_b\big].
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For $X\in\C$, $\pout_3[2\Re(X\phist)]=X\pout_3[\phist]+\bar X\pout_3[\overline{\phist}]$. The
second term vanishes: $\overline{\phist}$ lies in the angular mode $-3$, and
$\int_0^{2\pi}e^{-3i\theta}e^{-3i\theta}\dd\theta=0$. Since
$\pout_*(G)=\pout_3[\phist(G)]$, linearity gives
$\pout_3[w(t)]-\pout_*(G)c(t)e^{\lambda t}=\pout_3\big[w(t)-2\Re(c(t)e^{\lambda t}\phist)\big]$.
The function in brackets is supported in $N$, so Lemma~I.\ref{I-lem:uniform} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:active}\textup{(b)} bound its modulus by
$\Cp C_2\hat a(t)\big[\rhohat(t)+\hat a(t)+\kappa^{1/2}+\eta_b\big]$. Multiplying by $2\pi$
and using $\Cp C_2\le C_3$ proves the corollary.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Seeded Ring}

The approximate datum of Definition~\ref{def:approx} replaces its innermost ring by
a rearrangement $\Phi^\kappa$ of $\Phi$ whose deviation from $\Phi$ has an unstable
component of size about $\kappa$. We obtain $\Phi^\kappa$ by composing $\Phi$ with
the time-$\kappa$ flow of a divergence-free field $V$. This flow preserves area, so
$\Phi^\kappa$ is equimeasurable with $\Phi$, and $V$ is chosen with
$V\cdot\nabla\Phi=2\Re\phist(0)$, so that $\Phi^\kappa-\Phi$ is $2\kappa\Re\phist(0)$ to
first order in $\kappa$.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:seed}
Assume \textup{(N1)}--\textup{(N3)}, let $s\ge3$ be an integer, and let
$M=s+N_G+2$ be the integer of Theorem~\ref{thm:active} for this $s$. There are a
number $\kL>0$ and functions $\Phi^\kappa$, $\kappa\in(0,\kL]$, all depending only
on $\Phi$, and a number $\Cseed<\infty$, depending only on $\Phi$, $\Gone$ and $s$,
such that for every
$\kappa\in(0,\kL]$: $\Phi^\kappa\in C_c^\infty(\R^2)$, $\Phi^\kappa\ge0$,
$\Phi^\kappa$ is $3$-fold rotationally symmetric and equimeasurable with $\Phi$;
$\supp\Phi^\kappa\subset\NNm$;
$\|\Phi^\kappa-\Phi\|_{H^{M-1}}\le\Cseed\kappa$;
and $|\ellst(0)(\Phi^\kappa-\Phi)|\in[\frac\kappa2,2\kappa]$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $G=0$, $\lambda=\lamst(0)$, and let $\psi_*$ and $n_*=n_*(0)$ be
as in Theorem~I.\ref{I-thm:evans-simple} and Definition~I.\ref{I-def:eigendata}, for the
ring in ring units and $\ell=3$. By these definitions,
$\phist(0)=n_*^{-1}g_*(r)e^{3i\theta}$ with $g_*=\Mres_{\lambda}\kcoef_3\,\psi_*(\log r)$,
where $\kcoef_3(r)=3i\Phi'(r)/r$ and $\Mres_\lambda=(\lambda+3i\Omega(\cdot;0))^{-1}$
(Definition~I.\ref{I-def:sector-setting}). So the mode-$3$ profile of
$\phist(0)$ is
\[
\vphist(r)=n_*^{-1}\,\frac{3i\,\Phi'(r)}{r}\,\frac{\psi_*(\log r)}{\lambda+3i\Omega(r;0)} .
\]
Put $g_{\mathrm s}(r)=n_*^{-1}\psi_*(\log r)/(\lambda+3i\Omega(r;0))$ for $r>0$. It is
smooth: $\psi_*$ solves a linear equation with smooth coefficients
(Lemma~I.\ref{I-lem:sector}\textup{(3)}), $\Omega(\cdot;0)$ is smooth on $(0,\infty)$, and
$|\lambda+3i\Omega|\ge\Re\lambda>0$. Let $\chiseed\in C_c^\infty(\{a/2<|y|<2\})$ be
radial with $\chiseed=1$ on $\{a\le|y|\le1\}$, and put
$\psi_{\mathrm{seed}}=-2\Re\big(\chiseed(r)\,g_{\mathrm s}(r)\,e^{3i\theta}\big)$ and
$V=\nabla^\perp\psi_{\mathrm{seed}}$. Then $\psi_{\mathrm{seed}}$ is real, smooth,
$3$-fold rotationally symmetric and supported in the compact set
$K_V=\supp\chiseed\subset\{a/2<|y|<2\}$, and $V\in C_c^\infty(\R^2;\R^2)$ is
divergence-free and $3$-fold rotationally symmetric. Let $Y_\kappa=\flow_V(\kappa,0,\cdot)$ be the
time-$\kappa$ flow of $V$, with $\flow_V$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:flow} for the
time-independent bounded field $V$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:flow}\textup{(a)}, \textup{(b)},
\textup{(d)}, $Y$ is defined for all $\kappa\in\R$ and is $C^\infty$ in $(\kappa,y)$,
each $Y_\kappa$ is a $C^\infty$ diffeomorphism of $\R^2$, and $\det DY_\kappa=1$
because $\divop V=0$. Since $V$ does not depend on time, uniqueness for the flow
gives $Y_{\kappa+\kappa'}=Y_\kappa\circ Y_{\kappa'}$ and, since
$V(\Orot y)=\Orot V(y)$, $Y_\kappa\circ\Orot=\Orot\circ Y_\kappa$. For $y\notin K_V$
the constant path $y$ solves $\dot x=V(x)$, since $V(y)=0$; so $Y_\kappa$ is the identity outside
$K_V$.

Put $\Phi^\kappa=\Phi\circ Y_\kappa$. Then $\Phi^\kappa\in C_c^\infty(\R^2)$ is nonnegative and $3$-fold
rotationally symmetric. It is equimeasurable with $\Phi$: for $c>0$,
$\{\Phi^\kappa>c\}=Y_\kappa^{-1}(\{\Phi>c\})$ has the measure of $\{\Phi>c\}$ because
$Y_\kappa$ preserves area, and $\{\Phi^\kappa<-c\}=\{\Phi<-c\}=\emptyset$. Since
$\Phi$ is radial, $V\cdot\nabla\Phi=(V\cdot\er)\Phi'$, and
$V\cdot\er=-r^{-1}\partial_\theta\psi_{\mathrm{seed}}=2\Re\big(\frac{3i}r\chiseed g_{\mathrm s}e^{3i\theta}\big)$.
On $\supp\Phi'\subset\{a\le|y|\le1\}$ we have $\chiseed=1$, so
$(V\cdot\er)\Phi'=2\Re(\vphist e^{3i\theta})=2\Re\phist(0)$; off $\supp\Phi'$ both
sides vanish, because $\vphist$ is a multiple of $\Phi'$. Thus
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:seed-first}
V\cdot\nabla\Phi=2\Re\phist(0).
\end{equation}

We now expand in $\kappa$. Fix $y$ and put $f_y(t)=\Phi(Y_t(y))$. Then
$f_y'(t)=(V\cdot\nabla\Phi)(Y_t(y))$ and $f_y''(t)=(V\cdot\nabla(V\cdot\nabla\Phi))(Y_t(y))$,
so with $\Psi_{\mathrm s}=V\cdot\nabla(V\cdot\nabla\Phi)\in C_c^\infty(\R^2)$,
\[
\Phi^\kappa-\Phi=\int_0^\kappa(V\cdot\nabla\Phi)\circ Y_t\dd t,\qquad
\Phi^\kappa-\Phi-\kappa V\cdot\nabla\Phi=\int_0^\kappa(\kappa-t)\,\Psi_{\mathrm s}\circ Y_t\dd t .
\]
The derivatives of $Y_t(y)-y$ of every order vanish outside $K_V$ and are
continuous on $[-1,1]\times K_V$, hence bounded there. By the chain rule and
$\det DY_t=1$, for every $k$, $\|g\circ Y_t\|_{H^k}\lesssim\|g\|_{H^k}$
with a constant depending only on $\Phi$ and $k$, for
$g\in C_c^\infty(\R^2)$ and $|t|\le1$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:seed-first} and the two identities therefore give constants
$C$, depending only on $\Phi$, and $\Cseed$, depending only on $\Phi$ and $M$,
such that for $0<\kappa\le1$,
$\|\Phi^\kappa-\Phi-2\kappa\Re\phist(0)\|_{L^2}\le C\kappa^2$ and
$\|\Phi^\kappa-\Phi\|_{H^{M-1}}\le\Cseed\kappa$.

Finally choose $\kL\in(0,1]$ sufficiently small that
$\kL\|V\|_{L^\infty}\le a/8$ and
$\kL\|\ellst(0)\|_{(X^0)^*}C\le1/2$,
and let $\kappa\in(0,\kL]$. Since $|Y_\kappa^{-1}(x)-x|\le\kappa\|V\|_{L^\infty}\le a/8$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:flow}\textup{(b)}, \textup{(c)}, and
$\supp\Phi^\kappa=Y_\kappa^{-1}(\supp\Phi)$ with $\supp\Phi\subset\{a\le|y|\le1\}$, we get
$\supp\Phi^\kappa\subset\{\frac78a\le|y|\le1+\frac a8\}\subset\NNm$. The function
$\Phi^\kappa-\Phi$ is supported in $N$ and $3$-fold rotationally symmetric, so it lies in $X^0$.
By Definition~I.\ref{I-def:eigendata}, $\ellst(0)(2\Re\phist(0))=\ellst(0)(\phist(0))+\ellst(0)(\overline{\phist(0)})=1$.
Hence
$|\ellst(0)(\Phi^\kappa-\Phi)-\kappa|\le\|\ellst(0)\|_{(X^0)^*}C\kappa^2\le\frac\kappa2$, and
$|\ellst(0)(\Phi^\kappa-\Phi)|\in[\frac\kappa2,\frac{3\kappa}2]\subset[\frac\kappa2,2\kappa]$.
\end{proof}
