\section{The Relay Lemmas}
\label{sec:relay}

The relay needs three estimates: the rings outside the active ring stay
small; the unstable coefficient changes little after activation; and the
next outer ring receives a nonzero initial coefficient. Together with the
summability of the ring durations, these estimates let
Section~\ref{sec:bootstrap} carry the instability outward to ring $1$.
All times below are physical times. Ring $k$ is quiet on $[0,\tsh_k]$,
active on $\Phase_k=[\tsh_k,T_k]$, and debris after $T_k$.

The product summations and weighted error integrals are proved in
Sections~\ref{sec:proof-O}--\ref{sec:proof-kicktrue}.
Section~\ref{sec:param} chooses the parameters so that their smallness
conditions hold simultaneously. Each estimate uses only the solution up
to its current endpoint, as required by the continuity argument.

\subsection{Standing Assumptions}

Here $\sigma=\sigma(0)=\Re\lamref$ is the reference rate, and
$\sigma_i=\sigma(G_i)=\Re\lambda_i$ is the rate in ring-$i$ units
(Definition~\ref{def:regions}). Its physical growth rate is $A_i\sigma_i$.
All levels $1\le i\le n$ are considered. In applying
Section~\ref{sec:single} to ring $i$, the local notation $\sigma=\sigma(G)$
becomes $\sigma_i$ with $G=G_i$.

\begin{definition}
\label{def:standing}
We say that the \textbf{standing assumptions (SA)} hold if \textup{(N1)}--\textup{(N3)}
hold and the following objects are fixed:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(SA\arabic*)},leftmargin=*]
\item an integer $s\ge3$ and $\sigma'\in\big(\frac9{200},\min(\sigb,\frac34\sigma)\big)$;
  the corresponding regularity level $M$, constants and threshold functions
  supplied by Theorem~\ref{thm:active};
\item the integers $s_h=M-1$, $N_Q=\max(2,\lceil\Lam(s_h)/\sigb\rceil)$ and
  $M_h=s_h+N_Q+1$, with $\Lam$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:energy}; the associated
  quiet-ring constants of Theorem~\ref{thm:quiet} and the velocity-estimate
  constant $\Cvel$ at order $M_h$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer};
\item numbers $\delta_0\in(0,\dH]$, $\mu\in(1,\frac{17}{16}]$ and $\Tpin>0$; a
  number $\kseed\in(0,\kL]$, with $\kL$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:seed}; a number
  $\sbar\in(0,a/16]$ with $\mu\sbar^2\le\Gone$
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:inner-circ}); an integer $n\ge2$; and the objects of
  Definitions~\ref{def:datum}--\ref{def:clock} with these parameters.
\end{enumerate}
Under (SA), put $\rT(T)=(1+T)^3e^{-\sigma T/4}$ for $T\ge0$.
For every level $k$ for which $\ksh_k$ is defined, use the envelope
$\hat a_k(t)=\ksh_ke^{\sigma_kA_k(t-\tsh_k)}$ for $t\ge\tsh_k$.
For a constant $C_0\ge1$ named in each statement, let
$\rhohat_{C_0,G_k}$ be the transient in Theorem~\ref{thm:active}, and put
$\rhohat_k(t)=\rhohat_{C_0,G_k}(A_k(t-\tsh_k))$.
We say that the
\textbf{detuning hypothesis (Det)} holds if
$\sup_{0\le G\le \mu\sbar^2}|\lamst(G)-\lamref|\le\sigma(\mu-1)/(8\mu)$.
\end{definition}

Recall that
$x_k=\sigma_k/\mu$ (Definition~\ref{def:regions}). Hypothesis (Det) keeps the unstable eigenvalues $\lamst(G)$ of all
rings close to $\lamref=\lamst(0)$ (Definition~I.\ref{I-def:eigendata}). In the time of
ring $k$, ring $k+1$ grows at the rate $\mu\sigma_{k+1}$ and ring $k$ at the rate
$\sigma_k$; Lemma~\ref{lem:detuning} derives from (Det) that the first rate
exceeds the second by at least $\frac12\sigb(\mu-1)$.

Levels are integers in $[1,n]$; ring $1$ is the outermost ring and ring $n$,
the seeded ring, the innermost. All indexed ring quantities in this section
are functions of physical time. To apply Section~\ref{sec:single} on an
interval starting at $t_*$, we use its standard rescaling
$\theta=A_k(t-t_*)$ and the pulled-back functions
$\theta\mapsto w_k(t_*+\theta/A_k)$,
$\theta\mapsto h_k(t_*+\theta/A_k)$ and
$\theta\mapsto\eta_k(t_*+\theta/A_k)$.
For a quiet ring, $t_*=0$ and the Duhamel bound in Section~\ref{sec:single}
is $\theta\mapsto\maj_k(\theta/A_k)$; thus Theorem~\ref{thm:quiet}(c) gives
$\|w_k(t)\|_{H^{s_h}}\le C_Q\maj_k(t)$.
By the paragraph after Lemma~\ref{lem:regions-basic}, the fields and their
norms are continuous wherever defined, and so are $\maj_k$ and
$\maj^{\mathrm{in}}_k$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:inner-circ} and (SA3),
$0\le G_k\le \mu\sbar^2\le\Gone$ at every level, so all the eigendata
used below are defined and $\sigma_k\ge\sigb$.

\subsection{Quiet Rings}

A quiet ring is driven by the velocity field of the region inside it and by
the velocity field of the rings outside it. Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer-step} bounds these two fields by
the perturbations of the other rings and, for the inner field, by the inner field
of the next ring inward. With it, Lemma~\ref{lem:O} shows that,
while ring $j$ and the rings outside it are quiet, ring $j$ dominates them:
their Duhamel bounds are smaller than that of ring $j$ by a product of small
factors.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:detuning}
Assume (SA) and (Det). Put $e_\mu=\frac{\mu-1}{8\mu}$. Then for all levels $i,l$:
$|\sigma_i-\sigma|\le e_\mu\sigma$, $e_\mu\le\frac1{136}$, $\sigma_i\ge\frac{15}{16}\sigma>\sigma'$,
$\frac\sigma4\le x_i\le\frac{17}{16}\sigma$, and
\[
\mu\sigma_i-\sigma_l\ge\sigma\big((\mu-1)-e_\mu(\mu+1)\big)\ge\tfrac34\sigma(\mu-1)\ge\tfrac12\sigb(\mu-1);
\]
in particular $\sigma_i\mu^{i-j}\le\sigma_j-\sigb(\mu-1)/(2\mu)$ for $i<j$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:inner-circ} and (SA3), $0\le G_i\le \mu\sbar^2$, so (Det)
gives $|\sigma_i-\sigma|=|\Re(\lamst(G_i)-\lamref)|\le e_\mu\sigma$. As
$1<\mu\le\frac{17}{16}$, $e_\mu=\frac18(1-\frac1\mu)\le\frac1{136}$, so
$\sigma_i\ge(1-e_\mu)\sigma\ge\frac{15}{16}\sigma>\frac34\sigma>\sigma'$ by
(SA1) and $\sigma=\sigma(0)\ge\sigb>0$ (Proposition~I.\ref{I-prop:U}(a)); and
$x_i=\sigma_i/\mu$ lies between $\frac{15}{16}\cdot\frac{16}{17}\sigma\ge\frac\sigma4$
and $(1+e_\mu)\sigma\le\frac{17}{16}\sigma$. For levels $i,l$,
$\mu\sigma_i-\sigma_l\ge\mu(1-e_\mu)\sigma-(1+e_\mu)\sigma=\sigma((\mu-1)-e_\mu(\mu+1))$,
and $e_\mu(\mu+1)=\frac{(\mu-1)(\mu+1)}{8\mu}\le\frac14(\mu-1)$ because
$\mu+1\le2\mu$; with $\sigma\ge\sigb$ this gives the displayed equations. For $i<j$,
$\mu^{i-j}\le\mu^{-1}$, and the displayed equations with $(j,i)$ in place of $(i,l)$ give
$\sigma_i\mu^{i-j}\le\sigma_i/\mu\le\sigma_j-\sigb(\mu-1)/(2\mu)$.
\end{proof}

Recall that $\etin_k=\|\hin_k\|_{C^{M_h}(N')}$ measures the velocity field
of the inner rings after subtracting its monopole, whereas
$\etout_k=\|\hout_k\|_{C^{M_h}(N')}$ measures the velocity field of the outer
rings, both in ring-$k$ units.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:transfer-step}
Assume (SA), and let $t\ge0$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\arabic*)}]
\item Let $1\le i<n$. Assume that $S_{i+1}(t)\subset N_{i+1}$ and that the fields
  $h_i$ and $h_{i+1}$ are defined at $t$ (Definition~\ref{def:regions}). Then for
  every integer $s'\ge0$,
  \[
  \etin_i(t)\le \Cvel \varepsilon_i\big(\|w_{i+1}(t)\|_{H^{s'}}+\etin_{i+1}(t)\big).
  \]
\item Let $1\le k\le n$. Assume that $h_k$ is defined at $t$ and that
  $S_i(t)\subset N_i$ for every $i<k$. Then for every integer $s'\ge0$,
  \[
  \etout_k(t)\le \Cvel \sum_{i<k}b_{i,k}\|w_i(t)\|_{H^{s'}},\qquad b_{i,k}=\mu^{i-k}r_k/r_i;
  \]
  in particular $\etout_1=0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We use (SA2) for the order $M_h$ and constant $\Cvel$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer}, and (SA3) for $\sbar\le a/16$ and the ring
construction whose properties we recall next.
We use three facts. (a) $\omega_{\ge l}(t)$ is real and $3$-fold rotationally symmetric
(Lemma~\ref{lem:regions-basic}(3)). (b) $\int w_l(t)=0$ if $S_l(t)\subset N_l$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:regions-basic}(4)). (c) For real radial $g\in L^1(\R^2)$
supported in $\overline B_\rho$, the radial Biot--Savart formula of
Lemma~I.\ref{I-lem:radial-stationary} gives
$\KBS\ast g(x)=\frac1{2\pi}(\int g)\frac{x^\perp}{|x|^2}$ for $|x|\ge2\rho$.
For bounded $g$ this follows directly, with pointwise equality outside its
support by continuity. Truncation of $g$ and dominated convergence give the
general $L^1$ case, since the kernel is bounded on the support for these $x$.

(1) We split the vorticity inside ring $i$ into ring $i+1$ and the region inside
ring $i+1$; in physical units,
$\omega_{\ge i+1}(t)=A_{i+1}\Phi(\cdot/r_{i+1})+A_{i+1}w_{i+1}(t,\cdot/r_{i+1})+\omega_{\ge i+2}(t)$.
The first term is radial and supported in $\overline B_{r_{i+1}}$, and
$N'_i\subset\{|x|\ge\frac a4r_i\}$ with $2r_{i+1}=2s_ir_i\le\frac a8r_i$, so by (c)
its velocity on $N'_i$ is a monopole; the second term has zero integral by (b).
Hence on $N'_i$ the velocity of $\omega_{\ge i+1}(t)$ minus its monopole is the
velocity of the second term, bounded in $C^{M_h}(N'_i)$, in ring-$i$ units, by
$\Cvel \varepsilon_i\|w_{i+1}(t)\|_{H^{s'}}$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer}(2)), plus
$E_g$, the velocity of $g=\omega_{\ge i+2}(t)$ minus its monopole. The function
$g$ is $3$-fold rotationally symmetric by (a) and supported in $D_{i+2}(t)\subset B_{ar_{i+1}/8}$,
as $h_{i+1}$ is defined at $t$; so by Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer}(1), $E_g$ is
bounded in $C^{M_h}(N'_i)$, in ring-$i$ units, by $\Cvel \varepsilon_i$ times
$\sup|E_g|$ on $\{|x|=ar_{i+1}/4\}$ in ring-$(i+1)$ units. In these units $E_g$ is
$\hin_{i+1}$ (its monopole is $G_{i+1}x^\perp/|x|^2$ by the definition of
$G_{i+1}$ and Lemma~\ref{lem:inner-circ}), and $\{|y|=a/4\}\subset N'$, so the
supremum is at most $\etin_{i+1}(t)$.

(2) By definition, $\hout_k$ is the velocity of
$\sum_{i<k}\omega^{(n)}(t)\mathbf 1_{S_i(t)}$ on $N'_k$, in ring-$k$ units. By the
last assertion of Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer}(4), this is the sum over $i<k$ of the
fields induced on $N'_k$ by $A_iw_i(t,\cdot/r_i)$, and by its first assertion each
of them is bounded in $C^{M_h}(N'_k)$, in ring-$k$ units, by
$\Cvel b_{i,k}\|w_i(t)\|_{H^{s'}}$. Sum over $i<k$; for $k=1$ the sum is empty.
\end{proof}

In Lemma~\ref{lem:O}, $\bar t$ is a terminal physical time no later than
the activation time $\tsh_j$ of ring $j$; its hypotheses hold throughout
$[0,\bar t]$.

Under (SA) and (Det), for levels $i<j$ the mode of ring $j$ grows faster in physical time.
This lets its Duhamel bound control those of the outer rings. On an interval
$[0,\bar t]$ on which the fields are defined, the comparison uses just two
integral estimates.

Recall that
$\eta_i=\etin_i+\etout_i\ge0$ is the sum of the inner and outer field norms.
Thus the Duhamel bound in physical time is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:O-duh}
\maj_i(t)=A_i\int_0^t e^{A_i\sigma_i(t-v)}\eta_i(v)\dd v.
\end{equation}
For $i<j$, first factor out $A_j$ and use $A_i/A_j=\mu^{i-j}$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:detuning} then gives
\[
A_j\sigma_j-A_i\sigma_i
=A_j\big(\sigma_j-\mu^{i-j}\sigma_i\big)
\ge\frac{A_j\sigb(\mu-1)}{2\mu}>0.
\]
We first record a generic integral estimate. Let $F$ be any nonnegative
integrable function on $[0,\bar t]$ satisfying
$e^{A_j\sigma_j(t-v)}F(v)\le F(t)$ for $0\le v\le t\le\bar t$.
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:O-fast}
\begin{aligned}
A_i\int_0^t e^{A_i\sigma_i(t-v)}F(v)\dd v
&\le A_iF(t)\int_0^t e^{-(A_j\sigma_j-A_i\sigma_i)(t-v)}\dd v\\
&\le\frac{A_i}{A_j\sigma_j-A_i\sigma_i}F(t)\le\frac{2\mu}{\sigb(\mu-1)}F(t),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
since $A_i\le A_j$. Separately, $\etin_j\le\eta_j$ by the definition of
$\eta_j$, and $A_i\sigma_i\le A_j\sigma_j$ by the gap above.
Using these inequalities and then $A_i\le A_j$ gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:O-same}
\begin{aligned}
A_i\int_0^t e^{A_i\sigma_i(t-v)}\etin_j(v)\dd v
&\le A_i\int_0^t e^{A_j\sigma_j(t-v)}\eta_j(v)\dd v\\
&\le A_j\int_0^t e^{A_j\sigma_j(t-v)}\eta_j(v)\dd v=\maj_j(t).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Iterating Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer-step} now gains a small radius factor at
each outward passage. The geometric product estimates in
Lemma~\ref{lem:O}, proved in Section~\ref{sec:proof-O}, show that all the
outer quiet rings are controlled by the innermost one under consideration.
In particular their combined feedback satisfies
\[
\etout_j(t)\le\theta_j\maj_j(t).
\]
Here $\theta_j$ is the coefficient specified in that lemma; it tends to
zero with the radius ratio $s_{j-1}$ and is zero for $j=1$.
The lemma also verifies the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:quiet} for
these rings. The product weights needed to make this comparison uniform
in the number of rings are kept in its proof.

The next proposition estimates the Duhamel bound of a quiet ring $j$ as the
next inner ring $j+1$ becomes active and later saturates.
Its hypothesis (H1) is the localization hypothesis
(Loc) of Definition~\ref{def:loc}. While ring $j+1$ is quiet, (o) and (i) bound
$\maj^{\mathrm{in}}_j$ and $\maj_j$ by a multiple of $\maj_{j+1}$. After ring $j+1$ has become active, (ii) and (iii)
bound $\maj_j$ up to the activation time $\tsh_j$ of ring $j$; in
Equation~\eqref{eq:relay}, the first two terms in the bracket come from the
envelope $\hat a_{j+1}$ of the active ring $j+1$ and from its debris after
saturation. The remainder $\Xi_{j+1}$ accounts for the driving accumulated
before ring $j+1$ becomes active, on $[0,\tsh_{j+1}]$, together with the
transient and polynomial inner-field terms in its active estimate on
$[\tsh_{j+1},T_{j+1}]$. Its subscript refers to the inner ring whose
evolution supplies these contributions.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:relay}
Assume (SA) and (Det), and let $C_0\ge1$ and $K_1>0$.
There is a constant $\Crelay=\Crelay(C_0,K_1)$ for the estimates below. It
depends only on $C_0$, $K_1$, $\sigma$, $\sigb$ and the constants of (SA1)--(SA2), and not
on $\mu$, $\delta_0$,
$\Tpin$, $\sbar$, $\kseed$, $n$ or the level. Let $1\le j<n$, assume that the
clock is defined up to level $j+1$, let $\bar t\in[0,\tsh_j]$, and assume:
\begin{description}[font=\normalfont]
\item[\textup{(H1)}] \textup{(Loc)} holds at every $t\in[0,\bar t]$;
\item[\textup{(H2)}] $\etout_j\le\theta_j\maj_j$ on $[0,\bar t]$, with $\theta_j$ of
  Lemma~\ref{lem:O}, and $\theta_jA_j\bar t\le\frac12$;
\item[\textup{(H3)}] if $j+1<n$, the conclusions of Theorem~\ref{thm:quiet},
  applied by the rescaling above, hold for ring $j+1$ through physical time
  $\min(\bar t,\tsh_{j+1})$;
\item[\textup{(H4)}] if $\bar t>\tsh_{j+1}$, then
  $\maj_{j+1}(\tsh_{j+1})\le K_1\ksh_{j+1}$, and for
  $t\in[\tsh_{j+1},\min(\bar t,T_{j+1})]$:
  $D_{j+2}(t)\subset B_{ar_{j+1}/8}$,
  $\|w_{j+1}(t)\|_{H^s}\le2C_2\hat a_{j+1}(t)(1+\rhohat_{j+1}(t))$ and
  $\|\hin_{j+1}(t)\|_{C^{M_h}(N')}\le C_0\ksh_{j+1}\jap{A_{j+1}(t-\tsh_{j+1})}^3$.
\end{description}
Recall that the Duhamel bound of the inner field, with its monopole removed, is
\[
\maj^{\mathrm{in}}_j(t)=A_j\int_0^t
e^{A_j\sigma_j(t-v)}\etin_j(v)\dd v,
\qquad \etin_j=\|\hin_j\|_{C^{M_h}(N')}.
\]
Then $\maj_j\le2\maj^{\mathrm{in}}_j$ on $[0,\bar t]$, and:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item[\textup{(o)}] if $j+1<n$, then for every $t\in[0,\min(\bar t,\tsh_{j+1})]$,
  \[
  \maj^{\mathrm{in}}_j(t)\le \Cvel \Big(\frac{2C_Q}{\sigb(\mu-1)}+1\Big)\varepsilon_j\,\maj_{j+1}(t);
  \]
\item if $\bar t\le\tsh_{j+1}$, then
  $\maj_j(\bar t)\le2\Cvel \big(\frac{2C_Q}{\sigb(\mu-1)}+1\big)\varepsilon_j\,\maj_{j+1}(\bar t)$;
\item if $\bar t=\tsh_j$, then
  \begin{equation}
  \label{eq:relay}
  \maj_j(\tsh_j)\le2\Cvel \varepsilon_je^{x_j\Tpin}\Big[\frac{4C_2\delta_0}{\sigb(\mu-1)}+C_D(\Tpin)+\Xi_{j+1}\Big],
  \end{equation}
  where $C_D(T)=(2+2\Cstar T)^3T$,
  $\gamma_j=\max(\sigma_j,\mu\sigma')/(\mu\sigma_{j+1})$, and
  \[
  \Xi_{j+1}=\Crelay\Big(1+\frac1{\sigb(\mu-1)}\Big)\delta_0\Big(\frac{\ksh_{j+1}}{\delta_0}\Big)^{1-\gamma_j}\Big\langle\log\frac{\delta_0}{\ksh_{j+1}}\Big\rangle^4;
  \]
\item if $\tsh_{j+1}<\bar t<\tsh_j$, then $\maj_j(\bar t)$ is at most the right side
  of Equation~\eqref{eq:relay}.
\end{enumerate}
All times used are at most $\bar t$ or are determined by $\omega^{(n)}$ on
$[0,\bar t]$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The outer rings contribute only a small feedback. Indeed, (H2) and (M1) of
Lemma~\ref{lem:majorant}, in physical time, give for $t\le\bar t$
\[
\maj_j(t)\le\maj^{\mathrm{in}}_j(t)
 +\theta_jA_j\int_0^t e^{A_j\sigma_j(t-v)}\maj_j(v)\dd v
\le\maj^{\mathrm{in}}_j(t)+\tfrac12\maj_j(t).
\]
Thus $\maj_j\le2\maj^{\mathrm{in}}_j$.

It remains to estimate the field arriving from ring $j+1$. Before
$\tsh_{j+1}$, Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer-step}(1) and Theorem~\ref{thm:quiet}
bound it by $\Cvel\varepsilon_j(C_Q\maj_{j+1}+\eta_{j+1})$.
The growth gap of Lemma~\ref{lem:detuning} makes the Duhamel integral of
the first term at most $2\maj_{j+1}/(\sigb(\mu-1))$; changing from
ring-$j$ time to ring-$(j+1)$ time bounds the integral of the second by
$\maj_{j+1}$. These are (o) and (i).

After $\tsh_{j+1}$, split the physical-time integral at
$\tsh_{j+1}$ and $T_{j+1}$. During $[\tsh_{j+1},T_{j+1}]$, (H4) separates
the growing mode from its transient and the polynomial inner field.
The growing mode contributes the term $4C_2\delta_0/(\sigb(\mu-1))$ in
\eqref{eq:relay}, again by the growth gap. During
$[T_{j+1},\tsh_j]$, (Loc) and Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer}(3) bound the debris
contribution by $C_D(\Tpin)$. The earlier history and the two remaining
active terms contribute at most $\Xi_{j+1}$, after the common factor
$\Cvel\varepsilon_je^{x_j\Tpin}$ is removed.
The integrations establishing these bounds, including their uniformity in
$\mu$ and in the level, are given in Section~\ref{sec:proof-relay}.
Multiplying by $2$ gives (ii) and (iii).
All integration intervals end at the current time; the clock times used
when an interval is split have already been determined by then.
\end{proof}

While ring $k$ is active, the quiet rings outside it are driven by its growing
perturbation and act back on it through the outer field $\hout_k$. The next lemma
bounds this field by a small multiple $\eta_{b,k}$ of the envelope $\hat a_k$; this
is the form of the second part $h_2$ of the field in hypothesis (Field) of
Theorem~\ref{thm:active}.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:Oprime}
Assume (SA) and (Det), and let $C_0\ge1$ and $K_1>0$.
After enlarging the common constant $\Crelay(C_0,K_1)$ of
Proposition~\ref{prop:relay} if necessary, the estimate below holds. It
depends only on $C_0$, $K_1$, $\sigma$, $\sigb$ and the constants of
(SA1)--(SA2), and not on $\mu$,
$\delta_0$, $\Tpin$, $\sbar$, $\kseed$, $n$ or the level. Let $1\le k\le n$, assume
that the clock is defined up to level $k$, and let $t\in\Phase_k$ with $t>0$. Assume on
$[0,t]$: \textup{(Loc)}; if $k\ge2$, the hypotheses and conclusions of
Lemma~\ref{lem:O} for $j=k-1$ and $\bar t=t$ (ring $k-1$ is quiet on $[0,t]$,
since $t\le T_k<\tsh_{k-1}$), and $\theta_{k-1}A_{k-1}t\le\frac12$; the active
bound $\|w_k(t')\|_{H^s}\le2C_2\hat a_k(t')(1+\rhohat_k(t'))$ and the inner bound
$\|\hin_k(t')\|_{C^{M_h}(N')}\le C_0\ksh_k\jap{A_k(t'-\tsh_k)}^3$ for
$t'\in[\tsh_k,t]$; and, if $k<n$: the conclusions of
Theorem~\ref{thm:quiet} for ring $k$, after rescaling, through physical time $\tsh_k$,
$\maj_k(\tsh_k)\le K_1\ksh_k$, and $D_{k+1}(t')\subset B_{ar_k/8}$ for
$t'\in[\tsh_k,t]$. Then for every $t'\in[\tsh_k,t]$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:etab}
\|\hout_k(t')\|_{C^{M_h}(N')}\le\eta_{b,k}\,\hat a_k(t'),\qquad
\eta_{b,k}=\Crelay\frac{s_{k-1}^6}{\sigb(\mu-1)}\le\eta_b=\Crelay\frac{\sbar^6}{\sigb(\mu-1)}
\end{equation}
for $k\ge2$, and $\hout_1=0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $k=1$ there are no outer rings. Let $k\ge2$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer-step}(2), Theorem~\ref{thm:quiet} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:O} reduce the sum of all outer fields to the nearest quiet ring:
\[
\etout_k(t')\le2\Cvel C_Q\,b_{k-1,k}\maj_{k-1}(t').
\]
In the Duhamel bound for this ring, the mode of ring $k$ grows faster than
the kernel, by Lemma~\ref{lem:detuning}. Its transient and polynomial
inner field have bounded weighted integrals. Applying
Proposition~\ref{prop:relay} and integrating these three contributions
therefore gives
\[
\maj_{k-1}(t')\le
\frac{\Crelay}{2\Cvel C_Q}
\frac{\varepsilon_{k-1}\hat a_k(t')}{\sigb(\mu-1)}.
\]
Section~\ref{sec:proof-Oprime} checks the hypotheses and performs the
integrations; the same constant $\Crelay(C_0,K_1)$ works at every level
and is independent of $\mu$.
Multiplying the two bounds and using
$b_{k-1,k}\varepsilon_{k-1}=\mu^{-1}s_{k-1}\cdot\mu s_{k-1}^5=s_{k-1}^6$
proves \eqref{eq:etab}. The bound by $\eta_b$ follows from $s_{k-1}\le\sbar$.
For the seeded ring, the contribution before activation is zero because
$\tsh_n=0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Active Ring and the Hand-Off}

This subsection provides two of the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:active} for an
active ring $k$: hypothesis (Coef) on its running coefficient
(Lemma~\ref{lem:drift}), and, for $k<n$, the bounds at the activation time
$\tsh_k$ that give hypothesis (Init) (Lemma~\ref{lem:handoff}).

When we say that the conclusions of Theorem~\ref{thm:active}, or of
Corollary~\ref{cor:active-moment}, hold for ring $k$ on $\Phase_k$, we apply that
result with $G=G_k$ and $\kappa=\ksh_k$ at time origin $\tsh_k$. More explicitly,
with $u=A_k(t-\tsh_k)$ and $t(u)=\tsh_k+u/A_k$, its functions are
$w(u)=w_k(t(u))$, $h(u)=h_k(t(u))$ and $c(u)=c_k(t(u))$.
The spatial decomposition is into the vorticity on $S_k$, on $D_{k+1}$ and on
$S_1\cup\dots\cup S_{k-1}$.

By Equation~\eqref{eq:running-k}, the running coefficient $c_k(t)$ moves away
from $c_k(\tsh_k)$ through the field $h_k=\hin_k+\hout_k$. For $k<n$, the
inner contribution after activation corresponds to kick coordinates
$A_{k+1}(t-T_{k+1})\ge\Tpin$, and is small when $\rT(\Tpin)$ is small.
The outer-field hypothesis below follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:Oprime}.
In (ii), $q'$ stands for the amplitude $\qs$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:kicktrue}.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:drift}
Assume (SA) and (Det). Let $1\le k\le n$, assume that the clock is defined up to
level $k$, and let $\bar t\in[\tsh_k,T_k]$ and $\eta\ge0$. Assume:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item if $k<n$: $|q_{k+1}(t)|\le\Cmom\big(1+A_{k+1}(t-T_{k+1})\big)^3$
  for $t\in[\tsh_k,\bar t]$, with $\Cmom$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:confine};
\item if $k<n$: the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:coupling}\textup{(2)} hold,
  $\ksh_k\ge\frac12e^{x_k\Tpin}|\Ain(\Tpin)|$, and there is
  $q'\in\C\setminus\{0\}$ with
  $|\Ain(\Tpin)|\ge|\beta_k||q'|/(10|\lamref|(\mu-1))$ and
  $\mu-1\le|q'|/(10|\lamref|\Cmom\Ip{3}{\sigma/4})$;
\item $\|\hout_k(t)\|_{C^0(N')}\le\eta\,\hat a_k(t)$ for $t\in[\tsh_k,\bar t]$;
\item for every $t\in[\tsh_k,\bar t]$, $D_{k+1}(t)\subset B_{ar_k/8}$
  and the rings $i<k$ lie outside $B_{6r_k}$, so that $h_k$ and $c_k$ are
  defined there.
\end{enumerate}
Then for $\tsh_k\le t\le t'\le\bar t$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:drift-interval}
|c_k(t')-c_k(t)|\le2\rT\big(\Tpin+A_{k+1}(t-\tsh_k)\big)\,\ksh_k
+\Cls\,\eta\,A_k(t'-t)\,\ksh_k,
\end{equation}
without the first term when $k=n$. If $\rT(\Tpin)\le\frac1{64}$ and
$\Cls\eta\hat T_k\le\frac1{32}$, then hypothesis \textup{(Coef)} of
Theorem~\ref{thm:active} holds on $[0,A_k(\bar t-\tsh_k)]$ for the pulled-back
functions with $G=G_k$ and $\kappa=\ksh_k$. If $\bar t=T_k$, then
$|c_k(T_k)|e^{\sigma_k\hat T_k}\in[\frac{15}{16}\delta_0,\frac{17}{16}\delta_0]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $\kappa=\ksh_k$, $\lambda=\lambda_k$, $t(v)=\tsh_k+v/A_k$,
$u=A_k(t-\tsh_k)$ and $u'=A_k(t'-\tsh_k)$. By (iv), the fields are defined
on the required interval. Changing variables in Equation~\eqref{eq:running-k}
shows that $c_k(t')-c_k(t)$ is the sum of
\[
\int_u^{u'}e^{-\lambda v}\ellst(G_k)
\big(-h^\bullet_k(t(v))\cdot\nabla\Phi\big)\dd v,
\qquad \bullet\in\{\mathrm{in},\mathrm{out}\}.
\]
By Lemma~I.\ref{I-lem:uniform} and (iii),
$|\ellst(G_k)(-\hout_k(t(v))\cdot\nabla\Phi)|
\le\Cls\eta\kappa e^{\sigma_kv}$.
The outer integral is therefore at most
$\Cls\eta\kappa(u'-u)=\Cls\eta\kappa A_k(t'-t)$.

If $k=n$, then $D_{n+1}=\emptyset$ and $G_n=0$, so $\hin_n=0$.
For $k<n$, Lemma~\ref{lem:coupling}\textup{(1)} gives
$\ellst(G_k)(-\hin_k(t(v))\cdot\nabla\Phi)=\mu\beta_kq_{k+1}(t(v))$.
With $\zeta_0=\lambda/\mu$ and $\tau_a=\Tpin+\mu u$, the substitution
$\tauK=\Tpin+\mu v=A_{k+1}(t(v)-T_{k+1})$ gives the inner integral as
\[
e^{\zeta_0\Tpin}\beta_k\int_{\tau_a}^{\Tpin+\mu u'}
e^{-\zeta_0\tauK}q_{k+1}\big(T_{k+1}+\tauK/A_{k+1}\big)\dd\tauK.
\]
By (i), its modulus is at most
\begin{align*}
e^{x_k\Tpin}|\beta_k|\Cmom\int_{\tau_a}^\infty e^{-x_k\tauK}(1+\tauK)^3\dd\tauK
&\le e^{x_k\Tpin}|\beta_k|\Cmom(1+\tau_a)^3e^{-x_k\tau_a}\Ip{3}{x_k}\\
&\le e^{x_k\Tpin}|\beta_k|\Cmom\Ip{3}{\sigma/4}\,\rT(\tau_a).
\end{align*}
Here the first inequality uses $1+\tau_a+y\le(1+\tau_a)(1+y)$, and the second
uses $x_k\ge\sigma/4$ and the monotonicity of $I_3$. By (ii),
\[
|\beta_k|\Cmom\Ip{3}{\sigma/4}\le\frac{|\beta_k||q'|}{10|\lamref|(\mu-1)}
\le|\Ain(\Tpin)|\le2\kappa e^{-x_k\Tpin}.
\]
The inner integral is at most $2\kappa\rT(\tau_a)$, proving
Equation~\eqref{eq:drift-interval}.

Apply this bound to the pair $(\tsh_k,t)$, with $t\le\bar t\le T_k$.
The two smallness conditions give
$|c_k(t)-c_k(\tsh_k)|\le(2/64+1/32)\kappa=\kappa/16$.
Since $|c_k(\tsh_k)|=\kappa$, we obtain $|c_k(t)|\le17\kappa/16\le2\kappa$,
which gives (Coef) for $u\mapsto c_k(t(u))$.
Finally, at $t=T_k$ this gives
$|c_k(T_k)|\in[15\kappa/16,17\kappa/16]$, and
$\kappa e^{\sigma_k\hat T_k}=\delta_0$ by Definition~\ref{def:clock}.
\end{proof}

At the activation time $\tsh_k$ of ring $k<n$, its unstable coefficient has
modulus $\ksh_k$ (Definition~\ref{def:clock}). The next lemma compares $\ksh_k$
with the modulus of the coefficient $\cin_k$ produced by the kick from the region
inside ring $k$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:coupling}\textup{(2)}), and bounds the perturbation
of ring $k$ at $\tsh_k$ by $\ksh_k$.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:handoff}
Assume (SA) and (Det), let $K_1>0$, let $1\le k<n$, and assume that the clock is
defined up to level $k+1$. Assume:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item for every $t\in[0,\tsh_k]$, $D_{k+1}(t)\subset B_{ar_k/8}$ and the rings
  $i<k$ lie outside $B_{6r_k}$, so that $\eta_k$ is defined on $[0,\tsh_k]$
  (Definition~\ref{def:regions}); and the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:quiet}
  hold for ring $k$ through physical time $\tsh_k$, using the pulled-back functions;
\item for some $\theta_k\ge0$, $\etout_k\le\theta_k\maj_k$ on $[0,\tsh_k]$ and
  $\Cls\theta_kA_k\tsh_kK_1\le\frac14$;
\item $\maj_k(\tsh_k)\le\frac12K_1|\cin_k|$, with $\cin_k$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:coupling};
\item $\|\ellst\|C_QK_1\maj_k(\tsh_k)\le\frac12$.
\end{enumerate}
Then, in ring-$k$ units,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:handoff}
\begin{gathered}
\tfrac12|\cin_k|\le\ksh_k\le\tfrac32|\cin_k|,\qquad \maj_k(\tsh_k)\le K_1\ksh_k,\\
\|w_k(\tsh_k)\|_{H^{s_h}}\le K_1C_Q\ksh_k,\qquad \supp(\Phi+w_k(\tsh_k))\subset\NNm .
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $\maj=\maj_k$ and $c=\cin_k$. By (i) and
Theorem~\ref{thm:quiet}(a), for $t\in[0,\tsh_k]$, $D_{k+1}(t)\subset B_{ar_k/8}$,
the rings $i<k$ lie outside $B_{6r_k}$ and $S_k(t)\subset N_k$. So the hypotheses
of Lemma~\ref{lem:coupling}\textup{(2)} and \textup{(3)} hold, $c$ is defined,
and by Lemma~\ref{lem:coupling}\textup{(3)} the physical-time linear response corresponding to
Theorem~\ref{thm:quiet} for ring $k$ is
$\wlin=\wlin_{\mathrm{in},k}+\wlin_{\mathrm{out},k}$; at physical time $\tsh_k$,
\[
\ellst(G_k)\big(w_k(\tsh_k)\big)=c+\ellst(G_k)\big(\wlin_{\mathrm{out},k}(\tsh_k)\big)+\ellst(G_k)\big(w_k(\tsh_k)-\wlin(\tsh_k)\big),
\]
and $\ksh_k$ is the modulus of the left side (Definition~\ref{def:clock}). As
$G_k\in[0,\Gone]$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:inner-circ} and (SA3)), $\ellst(G_k)$ is bounded on $X^0$ (Lemma~I.\ref{I-lem:uniform}), so
it commutes with the integral defining $\wlin_{\mathrm{out},k}$, and
Proposition~I.\ref{I-prop:semigroup} gives
$\ellst(G_k)(\wlin_{\mathrm{out},k}(\tsh_k))=A_k\int_0^{\tsh_k}e^{A_k\lambda_k(\tsh_k-v)}\ellst(G_k)(-\hout_k(v)\cdot\nabla\Phi)\dd v$.
By Lemma~I.\ref{I-lem:uniform}, $\|\hout_k\|_{C^0(N')}\le\etout_k$, (ii), (M1) of
Lemma~\ref{lem:majorant} and (iii),
\[
\big|\ellst(G_k)\big(\wlin_{\mathrm{out},k}(\tsh_k)\big)\big|
\le\Cls\theta_kA_k\int_0^{\tsh_k}e^{A_k\sigma_k(\tsh_k-v)}\maj(v)\dd v
\le\Cls\theta_kA_k\tsh_k\maj(\tsh_k)\le\frac1{4K_1}\cdot\frac{K_1|c|}2=\frac{|c|}8 .
\]
Since $\ellst(G_k)$ has norm at most $\|\ellst\|$ on $X^0$
(Lemma~I.\ref{I-lem:uniform}) and $\|\cdot\|_{L^2}\le\|\cdot\|_{H^{s_h}}$,
Theorem~\ref{thm:quiet}(b), (iv) and (iii) give
\[
\big|\ellst(G_k)\big(w_k(\tsh_k)-\wlin(\tsh_k)\big)\big|\le\|\ellst\|C_Q\maj(\tsh_k)^2
\le\frac{\maj(\tsh_k)}{2K_1}\le\frac{|c|}4 .
\]
Hence $|\ksh_k-|c||\le\frac38|c|$, which gives the first claim. Then (iii) and
$|c|\le2\ksh_k$ give $\maj(\tsh_k)\le K_1\ksh_k$, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:quiet}(c) and (a) (the ring part is $\Phi+w_k(\tsh_k)$) give
$\|w_k(\tsh_k)\|_{H^{s_h}}\le C_Q\maj(\tsh_k)\le K_1C_Q\ksh_k$ and
$\supp(\Phi+w_k(\tsh_k))\subset\NNm$.
\end{proof}

Four constants will enter the bootstrap: $\Klo$ and $\Kup$ bound the
coefficient with which a ring starts, $K_1$ compares its initial Duhamel
bound with that coefficient, and $\Abar$ bounds the size of a quiet ring.
Their choices, made before the seed and radius ratios are chosen, are
specified in Definition~\ref{def:K1} in
Section~\ref{sec:relay-constants}.

\subsection{The Kick on the True Field}

We apply the scalar kick estimate of Proposition~\ref{prop:kick} to the
moment $q_{k+1}$ of the whole region inside ring $k$.

The sets $S_1,\dots,S_n$ are pairwise disjoint
(Lemma~\ref{lem:regions-basic}\textup{(1)}) and each $\flow_t$ is a bijection, so
$D_j(t)$ is the disjoint union of $S_j(t)$ and $D_{j+1}(t)$ for $j\le n$, $t\ge0$.

The quantitative comparison is Lemma~\ref{lem:kicktrue} in
Section~\ref{sec:proof-kicktrue}. Its inputs are the active estimates for
ring $k+1$, its earlier quiet-ring estimates when $k+1<n$, and confinement
of the deeper rings. Under its smallness conditions, the amplitude
\[
\qs=2\pi\pout_*(G_{k+1})c_{k+1}(T_{k+1})
       e^{\lambda_{k+1}\hat T_{k+1}}
\]
satisfies
$|\qs|\in[\frac{15}{16},\frac{17}{16}]\,2\pi|\pout_*(G_{k+1})|\delta_0$,
and the kick delivered to ring $k$ obeys
\[
\frac{|\beta_k|\,|\qs|}{10|\lamref|(\mu-1)}
\le|\Ain(\Tpin)|
\le\frac{3|\beta_k|\,|\qs|\,\mu}{|\lamref|(\mu-1)}.
\]
Thus a completed active interval of the inner ring supplies a nonzero
coefficient for the next outer ring.

The proof uses the scalar calculation of Proposition~\ref{prop:kick}.
The moment of the region inside ring $k$ splits into the moment of ring
$k+1$ and that of the deeper rings. Corollary~\ref{cor:active-moment}
approximates the first by the unstable mode, and Lemma~\ref{lem:drift}
replaces its coefficient by the value at $T_{k+1}$. This gives the principal
term $\qs e^{\lambda_{k+1}\tau}$ in kick time.
Theorem~\ref{thm:quiet} bounds the earlier history, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:confine} bounds the deeper moment and the
post-saturation field. The error estimates in
Section~\ref{sec:proof-kicktrue} show that their weighted integrals are
small enough for Proposition~\ref{prop:kick}. They also treat the seeded
ring, where the earlier-history interval is empty, by a short endpoint
limit. No later evolution of the receiving ring is used.

\subsection{The total time}

A ring starts with a coefficient of order $s_k^5$ and grows exponentially
until that coefficient reaches $\delta_0$. More precisely, if
$\ksh_k\ge\Klo s_k^5$ for $k<n$, Definition~\ref{def:clock} and
$\sigma_k>\sigma'$ give
\[
\begin{aligned}
T_k-T_{k+1}
&=\frac{\Tpin}{A_{k+1}}
  +\frac{\log(\delta_0/\ksh_k)}{A_k\sigma_k}\\
&\le\frac1{A_k}\left(\Tpin+
  \frac{\log_+(\delta_0/\Klo)+5\log(1/s_k)}{\sigma'}\right).
\end{aligned}
\]
The numerator grows at most linearly in $k$, because
$s_k=\sbar(2\mu)^{-k}$, whereas $A_k=A_1\mu^{k-1}$ grows geometrically.
These durations are therefore summable over all rings. The seeded ring
contributes at most $\log(2\delta_0/\kseed)/(A_n\sigma')$ when
$\ksh_n\ge\kseed/2$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:time} in Section~\ref{sec:proof-time} gives the resulting
bound $T_1\le\Tstar$, uniform in the number of rings.

The bootstrap also needs a time bound before a ring activates, so that
its weak outer field cannot accumulate for too long.
Lemma~\ref{lem:quiet-time}, in the same subsection, proves this bound using
only the lower coefficient bounds for rings that have already started.
Together with Lemma~\ref{lem:theta-quiet}, it controls the products
$\theta_jA_jt$ in Proposition~\ref{prop:relay} and
$\theta_kA_k\tsh_k$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:handoff}.
